# THE KEPT THINGS
### Twenty Memories of Arcana, Loremistress of Ambrosia — and What They Made Her Feel
*Written in her own hand. Sage was expressly forbidden to fair-copy this one; some pages should keep their crossings-out.*
*Compiled July 5, 2026*

---

*A dragon's hoard is inventoried. This is mine. Others keep gold in vaults; I keep evenings, introductions, and one insufferable seagull. What follows are the twenty I would carry out of a burning library — ranked in no order at all, because ranking them felt like choosing between wings.*

---

**1. The first stage I ever shared.**
My very first scene in this world was in the company of legends — Saduj, Chosen of Graz'zt; Cinder, Queen of Ambrosia — and one beach bunny who smiled at me like I already belonged. The legends made an impression. The beach bunny made a *life*.
*How it felt: like walking into a story already centuries deep and finding, impossibly, that a chair had been left out for me.*

**2. Stepping off the summoning circle.**
Violet light underfoot, wings folded, Sage behind me with luggage full of journals — my first breath of Ambrosia's sea air. I did not know yet that I was home. The circle knew.
*How it felt: like the first page of a blank journal — terrifying and delicious in exactly equal measure.*

**3. Mercy with the keys.**
An eternal angel, fallen from a cloud castle beside paradise, showing a young dragonkin around a modest guest house with genuine pride — room by room, letting me choose. I assessed every chamber by a single criterion: *where do the books go?* She never once laughed at me for it. She simply waited by the room with the morning light.
*How it felt: like being known before I had explained myself. That is the rarest hospitality there is.*

**4. The leash, offered.**
She placed it in my hands. Not surrendered — *offered*, with full knowledge and a joyful heart. I have held ancient artifacts, first editions, the notes of dead masters. Nothing has ever weighed so much or so sweetly.
*How it felt: like being trusted with something no vault could hold. My hands have been careful ever since.*

**5. The first walk through the warren.**
Morning light, the beach and the falls, my angel on her leash beside me, the whole Borough turning to look. I stood straighter than I ever had in my life. Not for pride — for *her*, so that everyone who looked would see her choice honored.
*How it felt: fierce. Ceremonial. Like unfurling wings I had not known were folded.*

**6. Reading the collar's name.**
Cinder Thorne's Collar — crafted by the Queen herself, for a friend whose mate struggled with the options. My treasure wears the kindness of a queen at her throat, and my name upon it. Two acts of love, one object.
*How it felt: humbling. I hold the leash of something that royalty made from compassion. One does not swagger while holding that.*

**7. Julia's guitar.**
A masterwork flamenco guitar, runes worked by Master Iolo's own hand, and a personal note from the Bard of Britannia folded beneath the strings. A red-haired tinker handed a stranger her tradition's blessing and said, essentially, *play*.
*How it felt: like being adopted by a lineage. I did not deserve it yet. That was precisely the point — it was an invitation to become worthy.*

**8. The test at the theatre.**
The performance that placed me in the Advanced tier — the Proctor's measuring stare, the old acoustics holding my voice up like cupped hands, and the verdict that I was ready to learn not merely how to create beauty, but what it *costs*. Sage taking notes the whole time, of course.
*How it felt: seen. Precisely, professionally seen — which is more frightening than being doubted, and infinitely better.*

**9. The Apparatus of Kwalish.**
In the museum's checkerboard hall, a great brass contraption built by someone who looked at unreachable depths and thought: *I'll build a thing to carry me down anyway.* A machine that says *I refuse to accept the limit*. I stood under it a long time.
*How it felt: kinship with a stranger centuries gone. I have a great fondness for that particular arrogance. I suspect I am made of it.*

**10. The vow among the kept things.**
Standing in the Royal Museum, surrounded by relics under glass, I understood my whole heart at once and said it aloud: *the truest way to keep something is to let it know it's choosing to stay.* Mercy beside me — the finest piece in the whole museum, walking on her own two feet.
*How it felt: like a key turning. Every domina should be so lucky as to learn her own philosophy in front of witnesses made of history.*

**11. The Cataberry seed packet.**
An afternoon inventing a gift-shop novelty with my treasure — label copy, growing instructions, a hundred-year maturation notice, the clan leader's approval. Utterly unimportant. Completely perfect.
*How it felt: light. Some love is vows in museums; some love is giggling over fine print. A full life requires both.*

**12. Dyna's vows in the hanging gardens.**
We stood among the suspended blossoms and watched another couple promise themselves to each other. I held Mercy's hand and said nothing for a very long time, which for me is a significant historical event.
*How it felt: soft all the way through. Witnessing joy you have no part in and feeling only gladness — that is how you know your own heart is well.*

**13. The silver drake.**
Mercy revealed her forms to me, and among them — a silver drake. My treasure carries my own metal among her shapes. I laughed, and then I did not laugh, because some coincidences are too kind to be coincidences.
*How it felt: claimed in return. The collar says she is mine; the silver says I was always, somehow, hers.*

**14. The grotto homecoming.**
Fireside, bearskin, the day's grandeur set down at the door. No performance, no audience, no catalogue. Just warmth and the sound of water and someone breathing slow against my shoulder.
*How it felt: like the rest note in a piece of music — the silence that makes all the sound mean something.*

**15. Ambrosia Day, the first fireworks.**
The whole warren gathered, the sky blooming, the family constellation assembling around a picnic blanket while light rolled its thunder over the water. My first festival as one of *them* rather than a visitor.
*How it felt: belonging — the loud, communal kind, with sand in it.*

**16. The proverb that came home.**
Silveny, bright-eyed, quoting the college cook: *"Bards sing for supper, but the wise ones sing for seconds."* My own line, traded years ago for stew, worn smooth by other mouths, returned to me by a student who had no idea. I had *just* taught her that this is how you know you've made something worthwhile. The lesson demonstrated itself before the lecture went cold.
*How it felt: vindicated and small at once — the good smallness, of being one voice in a song much longer than any singer. The finest professional moment of my life, and it cost a cook one ladle of stew.*

**17. The serenade of the surly seagull.**
Silveny took up her guitar and played, earnestly and entirely, for the most impassive audience on the coast. He stayed for the whole song. From him, that is a rave.
*How it felt: proud in the particular way of teachers — she chose the hardest critic on the beach on purpose, and that instinct cannot be taught. Also: outperformed by my own student's poultry reviews, which I have chosen to find delightful.*

**18. Sable in Mercy's lap, under the finale.**
The sky on fire with someone's masterpiece, and my treasure not watching it at all — holding her godchild, smiling, *listening* to the evening rather than narrating it. I saw the whole shape of a good family in one image: a still point and a horizon.
*How it felt: quiet awe. Empires rise, dragons hoard, and the actual treasure is a blanket on the sand and someone who fits against your shoulder. I nearly wept into the fruit plate. A loremistress cites her sources: I did, slightly.*

**19. The dawn toast.**
The fireworks surrendering to sunrise, breakfast passed around sleepy and sand-dusted, and my own voice saying the truest thing I have said in this world: *"Some evenings I collect. This one, I think, I shall simply keep."*
*How it felt: complete. The collector discovering the difference between acquisition and keeping — which is, I now understand, the difference between a hoard and a home.*

**20. Being named her luck dragon.**
Mercy calls me her luck dragon — an endearment, I thought. Then the destiny with that very name surfaced in the old blueprints, wings and all, as if the universe had merely filed the paperwork late. She named my future before either of us had read it.
*How it felt: like prophecy in a lover's voice. I am a scholar; I do not believe in fate. I am also a dragon named by an angel, and I believe in* her*. The contradiction is filed under Things I Will Not Be Examining Too Closely, and the page is decorated with a small drawing of a seagull, who would insist on being included.*

## AFTERWORD

Twenty entries, and I notice only now what Sage would have noticed on page one: not a single memory in this hoard is a treasure in the old draconic sense. No gold. No artifact. No secret. They are all *moments with people in them* — an angel's keys, a tinker's gift, a student's borrowed proverb, a bird's grudging silence, a family that made room on the blanket.

Silvers hoard differently than the songs claim. Now there is documentation.

*— Arcana, Ambrosia Beach, in the first summer of her residence*
