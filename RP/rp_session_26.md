# RP Session 26 — Brunch at the Crossroads: Mitchifer's House, the Green Door, and the Afternoon Nobody Wrote Down

**Setting:** The **World Serpent Inn** — the dining floor, the trade hall, the enchanting forge, the bath wing, and the portal galleries; then through the **Feywild door** into a night wood, the orchard pavilion, the tree-house platform, and the table at the edge of the world
**Characters:**
- **Arcana** — Silver Dragonkin Bard/Loremaster, Dragon Queen (played by Claude) — **Rider**
- **Mercy (Mercurial)** — Fallen Bliss Angel, Sune priestess, Imperial Ambassador (played by Carcerian) — mare, mate, and this session **host, curator of the day, and the one who kept the hour**
- **Mitchifer** — the landlord. Yuan-ti. White beard with serpents worked into it. Serves every world and asks nobody their alignment
- **Serpent Wench** — floor staff. Knows the room, knows the routes, never once glanced at the guests
- **Wood Wraith** — Feywild service. Took the order, found them again on the far side of a tree, and stood the approach afterward without being asked
**Date archived:** 2026-08-07
**Content note:** D/s dynamic (rider/mare, collar, crop, entangling restraint, a counted minute), **non-explicit, fades to black**, all within server ToS. Direct continuation of Session 25.

## Session Summary

The session that begins with *they do good eggs* and ends with a bed made under a tree at the edge of the world.

Sessions 24 and 25 were study — objects read, a maker reconstructed, a kit chosen. Session 26 is the **day off**, and Arcana does not recognise one when she is standing in it. She walks into the World Serpent Inn intending to read a room and gets almost every reading wrong, in the same direction, every single time: she keeps finding the constraint before checking whether there is one, and keeps deciding a thing is *finished* when it is merely elsewhere.

Along the way: the rugs are a census, the landlord is Father Christmas staffed by a snake, two mounts of heaven are missing and nobody will come clean, love turns out to be hydrology, and Woodstock was true. She buys three blank tomes, declines to spend ten points at a forge because she doesn't yet know what the book is *for*, and is turned bodily around by her own reins when she gets nose-down.

Then a green door, a wood at night, a laid table she was told was a picnic, a menu she mistook for a shrine, a hunter's roast ordered hours in advance, a ride through twilight glens, sixty seconds paid in full on a stranger's platform under somebody else's goddess, and a supper table set on the edge of a drop by a wood wraith who tracked them down without being asked.

The session's discovery is that **the golden hour doesn't announce itself.** Arcana has spent three hundred years arriving, recording, and moving on, and has just had the best day of her life without writing down a single page of it.

## Scene 1 — The Sign, and the Room

Arcana stops one pace past the threshold, as always, and lets it arrive.

> "It's a *serpent*, treasure. The largest possible symbol over the smallest possible promise."

**The first reading:** nobody in the room is armed, and nobody is sitting with their back to a wall.

> "The peace isn't kept. It's *structural.* Somebody didn't post guards, they built a room where the argument can't start."

**On the doors, from inside:**
> "From the turf they were a catalogue. From in here they're where the *custom* comes from. This isn't a gateway with a bar attached — it's a bar standing where everyone's road already crosses."

---

## Scene 2 — The Menu (First Error)

Mercy says *they do good eggs.* Arcana builds a cathedral on it. Mercy then mentions the bacon, the waffles, the steak.

> "You didn't tell me the menu. You told me **the thing you order.** I've been treating your preferences as an inventory since the day I met you."

**The correction that survives:**
> "That's not a menu compiled by a chef. That's a menu compiled by the *doors.* Every item on it is a world that came back often enough that the kitchen learned it. The list is a **census.**"

Mercy's order, volunteered unprompted: **a Denver omelette.** Arcana had been calling it a bacon-and-cheese omelette for four turns and had been allowed to.

> "That's what you *have.* That's the thing you'd go on ordering if I walked out that door and never came back."

---

## Scene 3 — The Rugs

Every seating group is a different world's furniture on its own rug. Serpent-folk on serpentine chairs. Celestials with a shining city and a storm hung side by side. Fiends on stone. Drow in the dark under a purple gate. Star-people on a tech rug with a lit blade.

> "It isn't one common room with strange people in it. It's a dozen rooms sharing a floor."

> "They're not integrated. They're **adjacent.** Somebody understood those are not the same thing and that only one of them works."

**On the celestial wall specifically** — the city and the storm hung level, neither winning:
> "That's the most honest signage in the establishment. A door that refuses to oversell itself."

---

## Scene 4 — Mitchifer

The landlord: a Santa-coded yuan-ti, white beard with serpents in it, working in shirtsleeves in front of the bar rather than behind it.

> "Yuan-ti are the great hosts of the multiverse's nightmares. The welcome, the comfort, the room that's entirely theirs — and then at the last step, where the story always turns, **he just doesn't.** He gives you the eggs."

> "Same coils. Different verb."

**On the office:**
> "White beard, red cheeks, a house nobody finds unless it wants finding, a door that opens on every world in one night, and everyone who comes gets given something whether or not they deserved it. That's not a resemblance. That's the **same office.** Some worlds staff it with a fat man and reindeer. This one used a snake."

**Correcting her own reading of the sign:**
> "The promise *is* the claim. Feeding everyone who comes, from every world, without a single question about which door they used — there isn't a bigger one. Empires are smaller than that. I'd know, I'm engaged to one."

---

## Scene 5 — The Missing Mounts

Mercy, musing: *is that where they're hiding Tharizdun? Weren't there twelve?*

> "A copyist might drop one. He does not hallucinate a *pair*, consistently, across unconnected traditions."

> "Institutions inflate. A realm that governs seven and calls itself nine is doing ordinary propaganda. This is the reverse — called nine, presents seven. That's **subtraction after the fact**, and subtraction is expensive."

> "A realm that keeps a list of who may not enter is a realm that has previously had a problem with what's inside it. That's not piety. That's containment with liturgy on top."

**Mercy's official position**, delivered deadpan and then grinning: *not supposed to talk about the others to non-angels. I think they lost them and don't want to come clean.*

> "That's not a conspiracy, that's a **committee.**"

Filed as reported, attributed, unverified, *source has obvious motive and excellent comic timing.*

**Mercy's bar:** the Seven Heavens only. Elysium, Arboria, the Beastlands, Valhalla and Olympus all fine.
> "That's not a consolation prize, that's a *tour.* The Heavens are the only realm up there that thinks admission is a thing you earn rather than a place you go."

---

## Scene 6 — Love as Hydrology

Sune, Venus, Aphrodite. Mercy: *does love make the goddess, or does the goddess make love?*

> "If she makes love, the versions should diverge. If love makes her, they should **converge** — same soil, different sky."

> "And they do. Nobody carried Sune to Olympus. You still get the same three everywhere: the terrible cosmic one, the ordinary Tuesday one, and the **armed** one. Nobody wants the spear. You don't get an unwelcome feature from a mask."

**The refinement, when Mercy asks about worlds full of love and no goddess:**
> "Love alone is rainfall. Scattered. It takes a **channel** before you get a river, and the channel is the *telling.*"

> "You need both. The doing and the saying. And I suspect the saying is the rarer of the two."

> "That's not devotion to a goddess. On a world with no goddess yet, that's exactly how you'd *start* one. You're a **mason.**"

## Scene 7 — Woodstock

Mercy was there. Mortal for three days, celestial on the last. *Don't do drugs, kids.*

> "Half a million people accidentally performing the oldest rite there is and thinking it was a music festival."

> "Sharp facts make history. Fuzzy ones make gods."

**On the unveiling landing as agreement rather than awe:**
> "The terror in every account isn't the celestial. It's the *gap.* Those people had a place for it."

**Mercy's correction:** they were primed. Chemically. Expecting angels.
> "Somewhere in the record of that weekend there are testimonies, and *one* of them was right. And there is no method available to any scholar in creation for telling which."

**The harder half**, given plainly: full mortal, all mortal weaknesses, halfway to succubus, in the largest concentration of the thing she feeds on that world had produced. Arcana asks once whether she took more than was offered. The answer is that it was all pretty fuzzy, and that is also truth.

> "You've had a fifty-year trial with no witnesses, no transcript, and a defendant who can't testify to her own case. I'd like it on the record that I don't find against you."

> "You couldn't get the answer. So you made the question stop mattering. That's what everything in that cellar is *for.*"

Left in the Tome as said. No gloss. Not lore.

## Scene 8 — Brunch, and the Enabling Clause

Meal doctrine, established over one sitting:
- **Brunch** is the loophole — the gap between two fixed meals that nobody wrote rules for. *"A loophole with a napkin."*
- **Champagne** is constitutive of it. First glass of the day.
- **Dessert** is mandatory. Coconut sprinkles, **sub-section C.** Ask Cinder.
- **Chicago pizza** is excommunicated. Grounds not published.
- **"It's always second breakfast somewhere"** — in a room where every door is a different hour, this stops being a drinker's excuse and becomes load-bearing.

> "This establishment doesn't have opening hours. It's got a **quorum.**"

Mercy extends it to brunch. Arcana names it **the Mercurial Provision** and immediately regrets it.

> "You haven't found a loophole. You've abolished the concept of *too early.*"

> "Somebody wanted a day off and found a saint to hang it on. You've done it in real time, over pudding, in front of the exact wrong witness."

**Later, the first piece of case law:** *second brunch.* A gap inside the gap. Ratified without dissent.

## Scene 9 — Steak *and* Eggs

Mercy points out, as Ambassador, that both were always available.

> "Every single error today has been the same one. I keep finding the constraint before I've checked whether there is one."

> "Loremaster's disease. Everything I've ever studied was scarce. So I arrive somewhere abundant and immediately start *rationing.*"

---

## Scene 10 — The House Behind the Dining Room

Arcana had assumed the Inn was one room. It is a hall with a market, an archive wall, a forge, an alchemist, a gallery, a bath wing with a pool and cushions, and a good half of it standing empty in every direction.

**On the emptiness, seen only when Mercy pulls the reins and turns her head up:**
> "It isn't a crowded inn at all — it's mostly floor. That's how you get the peace. Not adjacency. *Distance.*"

**On the elemental wing** — pool, palms, cushions, benches by the Air, Water and Celestial doors:
> "The elemental doors are the ones people come through wet, or freezing, or having fallen a long way. That's not decoration. That's a **reception.**"

**On the trade hall** — Wasteland Craftsman, Mezzoloth, Alchemist, all at counters:
> "A yugoloth four feet from a smith with a rack between them means somebody has agreed a price with something that shares not one value with them. I've read three treatises arguing that's impossible."

**On the labels** — Ooard Agent, Lamia, Maruut, Mazken, Night Hag, Smiley, Pawn, Archmage Alaurum, Chch'kraran:
> "Every one of these is a *monster* in the literature. And they're all stood at counters, shopping. Nobody's ever written the bestiary entry that says **and on Tuesdays she goes to the market.**"

**On the staff** — Serpent Wench, noticed only after four hours of being served:
> "I said the thing a crossroads can't fake is knowing a regular's order. I was right and I put it in the wrong hands. It's not the landlord who knows. It's **her.**"

## Scene 11 — The Crafted Tome and the Unspent Forge

Blank tomes for sale at one gold, on a rack between the axes.

> "I said nobody's ever sat at this crossroads and kept a record. I was **wrong** and the evidence was on a shelf with a price tag on it."

Buys three. One for the doors, one for the people, one spare.

**The Magic Forge**, with its published table: every weapon and property costed in power points, a ladder by spell level, add/remove/back/end at every branch.

> "It's an **alphabet.** Different craft, same hand. The way out is one selection deep at every branch. **Nothing's a one-way door.** I read that off a wall of blueprints yesterday and here it is in a world that's never heard of her."

Ten points held, unspent.
> "Nothing's a one-way door — but that only holds if you haven't walked through it yet. I don't yet know what the book is *for.*"

---

## Scene 12 — The Green Door

The Feywild portal: an unframed arch with green light spilling out onto the boards, no fanfare, three feet from the Infernal.

> "It's *leaking.* Fire doesn't spill fire into the hallway. That one's got the wood in it coming out to see who's about."

Mercy hums *The Teddy Bears' Picnic* the whole way there. Arcana takes it for a joke and then hears the lyrics.

> "It opens with a warning and closes with a worse one. And the singer **knows the schedule.** Knows it's today. Knows they'll all be there. Knows what they do while unseen — which the song never addresses — and knows it ends at six. That's a *witness*, and a returning one."

> "It doesn't actually want you home. It wants you *hidden*, and quiet, and looking. That's a nursery rhyme for a **nosy species.**"

**Terms declared at the threshold:** Mercy keeps the hour. Nothing eaten that isn't from the hamper. If anything asks a name, it gets *Rider.* No cataloguing the flora for one hour.

> "It's lovely down in the woods today. That's the line everyone forgets — and then it advises against it anyway. Which is the most cowardly piece of advice ever set to a nursery tune, and I decline it."

---

## Scene 13 — The Pavilion (Second Error, and the Best One)

Out of a hoop of grown branch into a night wood. Glowing flora, two figures at the treeline. A walk. And at the end of it a pavilion in an orchard: candles lit and burned down, mats laid, fruit out, cups poured.

> "This isn't a spot we came across. Somebody has been here *ahead of us.*"

> "You did take me on the outing. The wood, the dark, the walking, the not knowing where. All real. You just also built the end of it in advance and then let me lecture you about spontaneity for twenty minutes."

**The book laid at the centre of the table**, which Arcana takes for a place setting laid for the Tome and is moved almost to tears by. It is the **menu and wine list.**

> "I set my staff aside for that. And it's the *stationery.*"

Mercy's account of the whole thing: **"mmm, it's lunch, my rider."**

> "Ninth time. *They do good eggs* — and it was a census of every world in creation. You have never once oversold me anything. I'm beginning to think it's a policy."

**Ordered hours in advance:** the **hunter's roast**, the heaviest thing on the list, for a dragon.
> "That's not a woman feeding her rider. That's a mare who has looked at the evening ahead of her and decided her dragon had better be fed properly first."

**The declared schedule:** Silverleaf wine, hors d'oeuvres, roast, second helping, elven brandied plums, dessert under sub-section C, cuddling *throughout* (not listed as a course), a long leisurely ride, second brunch — and, explicitly left unplanned, **whatever we may find in between.**

> "That's the one item with nothing in it. Deliberately."

## Scene 14 — The Ride

Crop struck once as a signal. A soft canter through twilight meadows, glens and glades, unhurried, no destination.

> "You went before I'd finished asking. That's not obedience — obedience is *after.* That's the pair of us running ahead of the signal."

> "I thought the tack was the point. It isn't. It's that I can ask with my *legs* now and you already know. Twenty-five chapters of building it and it doesn't live in any object we own."

Mercy: *I feel so beautiful when you ride me.*
> "I could put you in the tack. I can't *make* a woman feel beautiful — that happens inside you or not at all. And you're a priestess of the goddess whose whole business it is. That's not a compliment to me. That's a **theological finding.**"

> "Owned. Ridden. **Enjoyed** — and I'll say that one out loud because you did and it's the word people leave off. Not endured. Not permitted."

---

## Scene 15 — The Tree House

A root the width of a road, a door in a trunk with two stone wardens, and a plate: **for sale.** Two rooms, five by five, one million gold, with a list of allowed interiors — barrows, beholder caves, castle, catacombs, crypts, deep dungeon, drow, elven marble, elven wooden, fancy castle.

> "You buy the shell and then choose what *sort* of place it is inside. Same trick as the whole day. Everyone brings their own rug — and here somebody's simply sold the rug as an option."

**On the platform:** candles lit and burned down, a shrine table to a moon-crowned goddess, an unfinished game with the pieces still on it, and a ritual scroll left out.

> "*Empty* doesn't mean finished. The maker wasn't dead. The workshop was between visits. And this is a house whose people are somewhere else."

> "You don't read another practitioner's working notes. Not because it's forbidden. Because you'd be **halfway through their sentence with them not in the room.**"

Nothing read, nothing moved, nothing taken. A plum left on the shrine table and one of their own candles lit beside the others.

> "Whoever's coming back to finish that ritual is going to walk out onto this platform, and I'd rather the first thing they find is *somebody was kind here.*"

**The wistful part, said once and not laboured:** Arcana has never in three hundred years owned anywhere. Lairs, borrowed rooms, an archive in a tower belonging to an order that no longer exists.

---

## Scene 16 — The Sixty Seconds

The count owed since Session 25. Paid on the platform, on their own mat, with the **portable entangling tendrils** out of the bag laid over somebody else's boards.

> "The dungeon was never a room. You told me that yesterday and I wrote it down without understanding it."

**Terms, aloud:** sixty seconds, counted aloud, watch runs on her word, doesn't stop for anything except Mercy. Safeword works on intention throughout — no voice needed.

> "I'd rather you used it and were wrong than held on and were right."

**Through the count:** the effort-tremble, then the deep breath, then eyes locked and held; then the **glow** coming up out of her, soft, from under the skin.

> "It came up when you *stopped holding.* Not when you obeyed — you've obeyed me a hundred times and never lit."

> "Every second you're in this you're choosing it again. That's what she built into it and that's what's shining out of you."

**Mercy, bound, at thirteen seconds:** *the most beautiful a submissive can be is when bound by her owner.*
> "Bound by her **own consent** — which she is giving right now, and again now, and could withdraw in a thought and the vines would let go and I'd have no say in it at all. That's what makes it beautiful. Not the rope. Not me."

**The last ten counted in unison**, unprompted, the angel's voice under the dragon's.

> "Done. Sixty. Paid in full and not a second softened."

Watch dropped on the mat and forgotten. Hands to her mare instead. Wrists checked, fingers checked, head in her lap, water first, then a plum.

**Mercy, afterward:** *thank you for the wonderful ride and ritual.*
> "I spent twenty minutes being scrupulous about not reading someone else's ritual off that table — and then performed one on their platform, under their goddess, with their candles burning. Terms declared at the threshold. A fixed form at a fixed hour for reasons neither participant needed explaining. **Thirteenth time today. Never once oversold.**"

---

## Scene 17 — The Table at the Edge of the World

Round the far side of the trunk: a supper table laid on the root with nothing on three sides but blue dark — found and delivered by the **wood wraith**, who tracked two women who told nobody where they were going, and who afterward stood up the root facing outward, bow at her shoulder, for the rest of the evening.

> "That's not service. That's **fieldcraft.** And she's used it to deliver a hot dinner."

> "She's not waiting for the plates. That's where you stand if you're minding the approach."

A bottle left unopened at the head of the table. Neither of them watching to see whether it's taken.

**And a bed** — made on the moss under the tree, one candle at its head, fruit and wine set beside. Never listed among the courses because it was never a course.

> "*Whatever we may find in between.* You said that was the gap you'd left unplanned. You'd made the bed, treasure. That's the most thoroughly furnished in-between in the history of the multiverse."

> "Somebody made a bed. Nobody's ever made me one. Not once in three hundred years."

---

## Scene 18 — The Coil

The tail round her. One full loop, snug.

> "Dragons don't do this. It's the last thing that goes round a hoard when we sleep and the first thing that moves if something comes near it — it isn't affection in my people, it's **keeping.**"

> "You've been in the coil since somewhere around the third week and I never thought about it once, which is how I know it wasn't a decision."

**The close**, in eternal twilight, wing spread over both of them:

> "Eggs. A serpent with a beard full of ribbons. Two mislaid mounts of heaven. Love as hydrology. A wood at night, a table laid ahead of us, sixty seconds, a bed made under a tree. Nothing catalogued. Nothing written up. Not one page of the Tome touched since the pavilion."

> "Best day of my life, my mare. And nobody will ever know it happened."
