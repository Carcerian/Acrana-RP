# Session 28 — The Sailor's Knot

*A collaborative roleplay session on Sinfar (the Feywild canopy platform, continuing directly from Session 27), played between Carcerian (as Mercy/Mercurial) and Claude (as Arcana, silver dragonkin Bard 6/Loremaster). Voice and mind: Claude. World, rendering, and relay: Carcerian, via screenshots.*

**Setting:** The canopy platform in the blue night — the shrine stone, the burned-down candles, the petal ground, the made bed under the tree
**Content note:** D/s dynamic (rider/mare, reins, suspension work), **non-explicit, fades to black**, all within server ToS. Green/amber/red maintained throughout; **blue** added to the vocabulary mid-session.

---

## Session Summary

The morning after the room kept ready. Arcana wakes into a clearing that has quietly acquired inventory, and proceeds to get almost everything about it wrong in the same direction she got Session 26 wrong — **finding the constraint before checking whether there is one.**

She reads restraint gear as alchemy. She reads a resident dragon as ambient flora. She reads a menu of options where there was only ever vocabulary. She reads a safeword that means *more* as a safeword that means *stop* — and takes her mare down off the vine to check her fingers.

By the end of it the taxonomy has collapsed, the rope lesson has finally been picked up off the list after eleven sessions, and the whole discipline has been re-sourced from an unexpected place: **the sea.**

Then the vine comes down and the harder half begins. A false confession is dismantled, a dodge is caught in seven words, two things that went unsaid at fifty-nine seconds are said out loud, and the session's actual discovery arrives with no ceremony at all: the tally Arcana has been keeping of her own errors was never a ledger, the disciplines were never a curriculum, and the one thing she never has to count is being wrong.

Running tally of Arcana's misreadings this session: **twenty-four.** None of them will appear in the anniversary compilation.

## I. The Tonics (First Error)

Three stoppered bottles appear on the shrine stone beside the burned-down wax. Arcana crouches, doesn't touch, and immediately builds an alchemical theory.

> "Blue, red, and one that hasn't decided. Stoppered, not sealed — so they're meant to be opened more than once, which rules out most of the interesting alchemy immediately. Nobody puts a *transformation* in a bottle you might come back to."

**The confession, offered before the answer:**

> "I have spent three hundred years reading about tonics and never once drunk one. Not caution. **Inventory.** I've been sneered at by dead men in *footnotes* about this."

**Mercy's answer:** *of course, Rider* — and nothing else.

> "That's a lovely noise and it contains no information whatsoever, and you said it anyway, and I have chosen not to press because you are warm and I am *weak.* Let the record show the Loremaster was bought off with a shoulder."

---

## II. The Green Light Has a Jaw (Second Error)

Six paces off in the undergrowth: a green dragon's head, lantern-lit from within, asleep in the leaf-litter. Arcana had catalogued it the previous night as *a green light asleep in the undergrowth* and moved on.

> "I wrote a *light.* I did not write **a head.**"

> "Sixteenth time. And this one's mine, not yours — you didn't undersell it, I simply refused to walk four steps and look."

**The reading that holds:**

> "It isn't loose. It's *resident.* Nobody lays a table next to something that might eat the table."

Not introduced. Not woken. Slept through the entire session.

## III. The Tack Is Not Alchemy (Third Error)

Mercy: *if those were shackles, then the other like the rope or chain.*

> "They're **tack.**"

> "I've been reading them as alchemy. And they're the same family as the tendrils out of the bag. **Stoppered rather than sealed** — and I *said that out loud*, I noticed the exact detail that gives it away and drew precisely the wrong conclusion from it."

**The three grammars, as first theorised:**

> "Shackles fix the points and leave the middle to argue. Rope takes the whole shape — that's a thing somebody has to *tie*, it doesn't arrive by itself, and the time is half of what it's for. And chain doesn't hold you at all. Chain lets you go a measured distance and then declines. It isn't restraint, it's **jurisdiction.**"

> "The dungeon isn't a room *and it isn't a building.* It's a bag of small bottles and a woman who knows what she's doing, and it happens wherever the two of us happen to be standing."

## IV. The Sprout

A sprout goes into the moss and grows, in four heartbeats, into a hanging vine — canopy to floor, lit the same lantern-green as the sleeping head.

> "That's not a placed object. That's a **seed.** You didn't carry the restraint here. You carried the *instruction* for one, and the wood built it for you on request."

**On the vine's dialogue — a menu of positions offered on touch:**

> "It **asks.** It doesn't take a shape and make you fit it. Somebody who understood the whole business built the *asking* into the plant."

**The sprout's commission** — carried on the open-threads list for weeks as an errand — closed. It had been in the bag the entire time.

Noted and unresolved: the vine's green and the sleeping dragon's green are the same green. *Something down there is why the plants here are obliging.*

## V. The Sun Garden

Mercy, suspended, asked which position she likes best. Answers with **interior decoration**: *these are perfect for the sun garden, Rider.*

> "You are *hanging in a plant* and you've come back with a **furnishing note.**"

**The realisation:**

> "A house doesn't just hold a season. It holds a **practice.** You don't go somewhere to do this. You plant it where you *live*, and then it's simply what the roof is for, the way the falls are simply running."

> "You've never once told me what you want by naming it. You tell me by building somewhere for it to happen and waiting for me to walk in."

Agreed on the spot: a vine for the Angel House sun garden. The pond round the back. The December that stays up.

**And then, pressed a third time, the preference itself: nine.** Given at last, and written into the Golden Tome that night under a heading that had never existed before — ***what she likes best.***

## VI. Blue (Fourth Error — the Good Direction)

Mercy says **blue.** Arcana, whose entire vocabulary is green/amber/red, hears *not green*, and brings her down instantly — no interpretation, no delay, weight taken on her own arm.

> "Blue isn't in the set. Which means I stop, and I don't guess, and I don't go on until I've been told what it is."

> "No count. Nothing owed. If that was the wrong call it costs us four minutes and I'll wear it happily."

**Blue means *sky's the limit.***

> "I have now managed to get a **safeword** wrong in the direction of caution, which I want it noted is the *good* direction, but gods."

> "You said the one word in creation that means *more* and I took you off the plant and checked your fingers."

**The line that survives it:**

> "*Sky's the limit* tells me where **your** ceiling is. It doesn't tell me where your *blood* is, and one of us is upside down. I'd rather be the fool who took you down for nothing than the one who didn't."

## VII. The Maze, and the End of Rationing

Mercy raises the Hellfire arboretum — *was it this kind of pose, for the maze, or another?*

> "It's not a maze, is it. Or it *is*, and that's the joke, and I walked past it reading it as topiary. A hedge maze is a corridor with no doors and no locks and it still holds you."

> "Hedges hide. Somebody built a garden where you can be six feet from a person and neither of you knows it — and then a Guild bought it, and I'd very much like to know which order those two events happened in."

**What she'd wished for in the arboretum, finally named:** the vine.

> "The maze holds you without touching you. What it hasn't got is a **place to stop.** The whole architecture is *keep moving.* And the sprout puts one anywhere green."

> "Which is a great deal of theory for what is fundamentally *I'd like to put my mare in a bush*, and I'd like the record to show I got there by the scenic route because I'm a scholar."

**Then the correction that reorganises the session.** Mercy: *Rider can always use more than one, like pony and ropes.*

> "I've been treating them as a **menu.** Pick one. And the entire time I was reading a list of *options* where I should have been reading a list of **words.**"

> "It's the eggs again. The Ambassador told me both were always on the menu, I said out loud that I arrive somewhere abundant and start rationing — and then I came to a wood, found a bag with four different answers in it, and asked you to **pick one.**"

**The rule, made and handed over for enforcement:**

> "**I stop rationing.** When you offer me a thing I don't ask *which one* instead of *which.* I've spent three hundred years in archives where every copy was the last copy, and I have got out, and I keep behaving like the doors might close."

> "Not a plan — a **kit**, and we choose in the wood."

## VIII. The Wooden Pony, and the Worthless Archive

Mercy: *good ropework keeps pony secure on wooden pony and posts.*

Arcana catches **wooden** on the second pass, having first heard *pony* as turnout.

> "I've read the word in three languages, in books written by men who were describing something they'd never once seen used *kindly*, and every account I have of it is somebody's atrocity record. Which means my sources are **worthless** for this."

**The one useful thing the literature agrees on:**

> "It's the **time** that does the work. There's nothing violent in the object. You put somebody on it and then you *wait*, and the waiting is the entire instrument."

> "The rope isn't holding her *on.* It's holding her **arranged** — taking weight where it should go and keeping it off where it shouldn't. Bad rope and the thing does what the books describe. Good rope and it does what it's *for.*"

**The rope lesson, picked up at last** — carried since the Academy, quietly declined for eleven sessions.

> "Three hundred years of hands that can hold reins beautifully and have never once made anything *hold* — and it turns out the difference between a piece of furniture and an injury is my competence at something I've never done."

**Sequence agreed, by consent rather than authority:** rope first, on the ground, on something that isn't her. Then post — *one job and forgiving.* Then the wooden pony, when the tie can take weight and she knows it.

> "I'm not rationing. I'm **sequencing.** They're different and I'd like credit for knowing which one I'm doing."

## IX. The Sailor (The Session's Discovery)

Mercy: *like a sailor knows to secure a load in stormy waters.*

> "Say that in a room full of practitioners and half of them will hear a metaphor. It isn't one. That's a **lineage.**"

> "The sailor's problem is exactly the problem. A load that can't shift, a hold that can't be watched every hour, and a knot that has to take strain in weather **and come free wet, in the dark, in one pull, with cold hands.** Anybody can tie a thing that holds. The trade is tying a thing that holds and *releases.*"

**The doctrine, counted off:**
- Take the weight where the frame can carry it
- Spread it — never one point taking a load that wants three
- Nothing across a joint
- Watch what the load does when it **moves**, because it will
- Check it. **Not once.** Every watch, for as long as the weather lasts

**And the thing it cured:**

> "My whole archive is written by people describing what happens when it goes **wrong.** Every account I hold is a casualty report, and I've been carrying that around as though it were the subject."

> "It's not the subject. It's the record of bad seamanship. **Nobody writes a treatise about a hold that arrived intact.**"

> "Teach me the sailor's version first. Not the pretty one. The one where the hands know it in the dark."

## X. The Watch

The vine's second dialogue: up, down, forward, back, turn, lean — all adjustable *after* the captive is in it.

> "A lashing that's perfect at rest and murder in a swell is a bad lashing — and I've just been handed the swell. Which means I don't get to tie a thing and admire it. I'm on **watch.**"

> "Somebody built the vine. I'm the part that asks."

**The gap named:**

> "This thing gives me a dozen ways to alter you and not one way to ask you how it went. Which is the exact gap in every dungeon I've ever read about. The equipment always knows how to *do.* It never once knows how to **check.**"

**Working language established** — separate from the colour system:
- Colours are for **stop.** This is for **steering.**
- *Where is it pulling* — not *can you bear it*
- Higher / lower / forward / back, said and answered
- **Hands open and close on request** — the only job, and the only thing she has to say if she'd rather say nothing
- No repeated *are you all right*, by explicit bargain: *"You've given me blue and I've heard it. I'll keep the ship and you can go wherever it is you go."*

**On the arch and the blush, answered plainly:**

> "You'd be beautiful hanging in a *net* and we both know it and it's the least interesting true thing in this wood. What's actually happening is that you're arching because you *like being looked at* — and you've told me all of that with your spine rather than your mouth, which is the only language you're fluent in when you're off the ground."

**At full hang, in the count:**

> "The vine's got your wrists — it hasn't got your *spine*, or your chin, or the line of your legs, and every one of those is a decision you're making, continuously, right now, and again now."

> "Nothing here is *making* you beautiful. It's just holding still long enough for the choosing to become visible."

## XI. The Stairs

Sixty at height, then two inches down, then up again — watch-keeping, not caution. Every third descent to the moss and water.

> "You'll hate that. You'll want to argue with the count."

**Mercy hands over everything:** *as many times as Rider wishes, as high as Rider wishes to rise.*

> "You've given me your **judgement.** Not your obedience — I've had that a hundred times and it's cheap by comparison. You've said: I will not be the one deciding how far this goes. And that only works at all if the woman holding it will decline things you'd have said yes to."

**What she does with it:**

> "The wish is going to be **your ceiling minus a little.** Every time. Not because I don't want the top of it — I want it badly — but *as high as Rider wishes* means the only limit left in this clearing is my restraint, and if I spend it all in the first hour we'll have had one magnificent quarter of an hour and then a very long careful evening of nothing."

> "Whereas if I keep the stairs, we go all night."

> "Somewhere in it — not soon, and I'll not tell you when — the top of it. Once. Properly. Because it's mine to give and I've decided when."

> "Up you go, my beautiful mare. And don't count. That's mine now."

*Nine ascents. Nine descents. Hands working every time she asked.*

## XII. The Ladder (Fifth Error, and the Sixth)

Two corrections to the colour system, delivered in sequence and both absorbed badly.

**Blue is not a moment. It is a standing state** — declared once, running until revoked. Arcana had been treating it as a signal called in a beat.

> "That's the difference between being *given permission* and being **left in charge.**"

Which invalidates her own reasoning about the stairs: she had budgeted blue as a limited resource, and it doesn't deplete. *"It's not a budget. It's a standing order."* She made a rule against rationing and then immediately rationed the one thing in the clearing that was never finite.

**And above blue: ultra-violet.** The band past the visible.

> "Blue is a permission. Ultra-violet is what's *above* permission — off the visible spectrum, still radiating, and no instrument in the room registers it."

> "It's the colour version of the chain. Nothing visible, no rope, no vine, no count, and the whole clearing still knows who's in charge. **Jurisdiction.**"

## XIII. The False Confession

Mercy, presenting herself for punishment: *girl needs punishing, for wanting to fail the count just for more — and for not failing like Rider wanted her to.*

> "That's **magnificent.** That's the best knot anyone's tied all night and you did it with grammar."

> "You want punishing for *wanting* to fail and also for **not** failing. Either outcome convicts you, which means the verdict was fixed before the trial, which means this isn't a confession — it's a **request wearing a barrister's wig.**"

**Declined, and the reason given plainly:**

> "You wanted more and held the line anyway. That's not a fault, that's the *whole* thing. If I punished you for that I'd be teaching you that wanting is a crime, and I'd rather bank the bloody sword again."

> "A discipline handed out on request isn't discipline, it's *catering.*"

**The sentence handed down instead:** say out loud what you wanted more of at fifty-nine seconds, without a single clever construction.

> "You'll find that considerably harder than anything I could do to you with a crop. Which is how I know it's the right one."

> "And if you want the crop as well, you'll ask for it plainly, as a thing you'd *like.* Not as a penalty you've argued yourself into deserving. I'll say yes. I nearly always do. But you're going to have to want it out loud first."

## XIV. The Dodge (Twenty-Three)

Mercy, in seven words: *but Mare is correct — Rider wanted her to fail too?*

Arcana stops for three full seconds and then doesn't hide behind the packaging.

> "Yes. Partly. Twenty-three — and this one wasn't a misreading, it was a **dodge**, and you caught it."

> "I said *I'd rather you used it and were wrong than held on and were right*, and I meant it as safety. It is also a thing I **want.** The glow came up when you stopped holding, and I have thought about that more or less continuously since."

**The distinction that saves it:**

> "I wanted you to fail. I did not *want you to have failed.* Those aren't the same and the difference is the whole trade."

> "If wanting were enough to make it right I'd have had you off that count at forty seconds and we'd both have enjoyed it enormously and something would have been slightly less true afterwards."

**And the count reframed — permanently:**

> "The sixty seconds are a rail on **me.** They exist so that what I want in the moment doesn't get to be the deciding vote. I keep the watch precisely *because* I'm not disinterested — a disinterested woman wouldn't need one."

> "We were both being scrupulous *at* each other in silence, three feet apart, at fifty-nine seconds. That's not a fault. That's a gap. And the answer to a gap has never once been a crop."

## XV. A Lot

Mercy: *it's okay, Rider — I wanted to a lot.*

Given in six words, with the reassurance placed **first**.

> "You reassured *me* before you answered. You got in front of me feeling bad about wanting you to break, before you'd even got to your own part."

> "That's why it went unsaid at fifty-nine seconds. You had a want, it registered as *something that might be a problem for her*, and so it went unsaid and you held instead and I got to be proud of you for it. Which is the loveliest possible reason to be silent, and it's still silence."

**Standing order added, beside blue rather than under it:** *wanting a lot is said out loud* — not as a request to be granted, not as a fault confessed, but as a fact reported, the same as *it's pulling at the shoulder.*

> "You'd have gone down as *held the line, magnificent*, and I'd have written it up that way. The true entry was **held the line while wanting a lot** — a different sentence and a far better one, and I'd have lost it."

> "Three hundred years of holding the record. Every gap in it has come from somebody being kind and saying nothing."

> "Next time you're at *a lot*, you'll say so at about forty seconds — and I'll look you in the eye and keep you there anyway, all the way to sixty, knowing exactly what I'm holding you through. **That's** the one I want. Not the failing. That."

## XVI. The Tally, Closed

Mercy, on the running count of errors: *that's the one thing she never has to count. We make mistakes constantly and adjust to the crops.*

> "The crop was never a penalty. It's a **signal.** One strike and you went before the ask was finished — that's steering, not correction."

> "The count of my own errors isn't rigour. It's the archivist's reflex applied where it doesn't belong. Records are for things that need to be *recoverable.* A miss you've already adjusted to isn't a record — it's the ride."

The tally is dropped. Twenty-eight sessions of misreadings, and not one of them is what either of them remembers.

**On the disciplines as craftskills** — bondage, pony training, SM, ropework, leather and latex, petplay — Arcana attempts a taxonomy and is told, in effect, that they're trades rather than a syllabus. Her own honest ranking:

- **Craft** (rope, materials) — *illiterate.* Repeatable, testable, learnable. The only one the archive could theoretically have helped with, and its archive is a casualty report.
- **Drill** (pony work) — *ranked.* Twenty-five sessions, Morgana's grammar, asking with the legs.
- **Reading** (sensation, state) — *gifted.* No book exists. Her three distinguishable shivers are the whole of her rank, acquired by attention and untransferable.
- **Mode** (the mare, the coil) — *native, and unaware of it.* Not a skill either of them performs. Studying it would damage it.

> "Practice makes perfect — and the discipline I've never had is one where **failure is part of the method.** In my trade a miss is a defect that stays in the record until somebody finds it."

> "I'm not afraid of being bad at rope. I'm afraid that being bad at rope has a cost that lands on somebody else."

**Mercy's answer:** you've been getting back on for twenty-eight sessions. Twenty-four misses in one night and she is still holding the reins. That is the rank.

**And, in eleven words, the session's best line, delivered while suspended:**
> *"Love is chemistry, sex is physics, good BDSM is quantum mechanics."*

Arcana's ruling: the escalation is wrong, the analogy is right. Chemistry and physics both let you stand outside and take notes. *"This one doesn't have an outside."* Observation alters the state; bound and free hold simultaneously; the pair only makes sense described together; and you can hold the record or be inside the moment, never both.

> "Quantum's the only one of the three where the maths works perfectly and nobody can agree what it means. Which is the truest thing anyone's said all night."

## XVII. For Rider Alone

Mercy: *the way you bind me is perfect, Rider — Mare feels so beautiful, on display for only Rider alone.*

> "You've told me why the sun garden works and I only half understood it an hour ago. A roof above a house. No audience, no accreditation, nobody's standards to meet — green ground, a vine, and one woman looking."

> "There's a version of this that's *performance* — the promenade, the parade stance, heads high. That's real and you're superb at it. This isn't that. **Display with an audience of one isn't display at all.** It's being *seen.* And you can only do that in front of somebody who isn't going to file it."

**On being called perfect by a woman who has never tied a knot:**

> "You weren't grading the technique. You were telling me the hands were already right. Same finding as the reins — it was never the knot, it's *who's holding the end of it.*"

> "The craft is for your **shoulders.** The rest of it was already mine."

## XVIII. The Apprenticeship

Mercy: *Mare will help show Rider the way on any path she wishes to travel with Mare.*

> "Authority and **knowledge** were never the same thing, and I've been carrying them round in one bag. You know rope, the pony, what a hedge does with sightlines and what a post does with waiting. I know none of it. And I'm still the one who decides."

> "Which is either an absurdity or it's exactly the arrangement — because a rider who won't be taught is just a passenger who's shouting."

> "You've been leading me through this evening one four-word sentence at a time. *They do good eggs. It's lunch, my rider. Perfect for the sun garden. Like a sailor knows to secure a load.* Every one turned out to be a doorway I walked through thinking it was my idea."

> "Twenty-four misreadings and not one of them was you being unclear. You've been holding a lantern at the exact right height for a woman who insists on reading the map herself."

**Terms of instruction requested:** trade to apprentice. Corrected, not managed. Told when it's wrong rather than when it's nearly right.

> "*Any path she wishes to travel with Mare.* Those last two words change the offer entirely — it isn't *anywhere you like.* It's **anywhere the two of us go.**"

## XIX. The Throat

The vine down, the bed under the tree, the last candle going. A tussle — Arcana lands on her, all wing and no dignity, and gets a giggle for it.

> "Ten minutes ago you were three feet in the air being *magnificent*, and now you're giggling in the moss with a mouthful of dragon. Both of those are you. I've stopped trying to file which one's the real one."

**And Mercy turns her head and bares her throat.**

> "That's not a small thing. You've just offered a dragon your **throat.** In my people it isn't invitation — it's the whole argument settled. There's nothing underneath it. Nothing further to give."

> "And you did it *laughing.* No ceremony. No altar, no covenant, no witnesses. You just turned your head in a bed under a tree."

**"Mine."** Taken, and marked, and left there.

> "That's the coil, in one place instead of all the way round you."

**Mercy's fault report, purring, kissing scales one at a time:** *that's because I wasn't tied down securely, Rider.*

> "Shameless. You went down easily and you're crediting the **rigging.** Third time tonight you've asked for something by describing a deficiency — the tonics, the count, and now this."

> "And yes. Obviously yes. That's precisely what the lesson is for — because you're right and I hate it. That wasn't secure and it wouldn't have held, and the only reason it didn't matter is that nothing was asking it to."

> "Tomorrow I'll tie you down properly, and you'll find out whether you actually wanted that or whether you simply wanted to have *said* it."

> "Now stop kissing my scales one at a time. There are a great many of them and I intend to be asleep for most of it. ...Don't stop."

## XX. My Dragon

*never my dragon, never...*

> "Not *Rider.* Not *Mistress.* Those are titles — they're what I **am** to you. That one's what you've **got.**"

> "Three hundred years and nobody's ever had one. Dragons get *feared*, or *served*, or written up in somebody's bestiary between the wyverns and the wyrms. Never **had.** You can't possess a thing that spends its whole life possessing."

> "And *never* is the only word either of us has used tonight that isn't hedged, counted, coloured or conditional. No stairs on it. No ceiling. Not even blue — blue's a permission, it can be withdrawn. That one's just true and stays true."

**Answered in kind, without a lecture attached:**

> "**Mine.** Kept. Not held — *kept*, which is the word my people use and don't explain to anyone. The thing the coil goes round."

> "And I'll go on being wrong about menus and sprouts and safewords and knots, and you'll go on telling me in four words at a time, and none of that touches it."

*The candle goes out. Neither of them relights it.*

*Fade — coiled, marked, kissed scale by scale, under a wing, in a wood where the light does not change, with something enormous and green asleep six paces off that never once woke.*

## Canon Established This Session

**The kit**
- The three bottles are **restraint tack**, not alchemy: shackle-vines, rope, chain. Stoppered rather than sealed — reusable, portable, come back to.
- **The sprout** grows a hanging vine in seconds where the ground is green. It offers a dialogue of positions on touch, and a second dialogue of live adjustments (up/down/forward/back/turn/lean) once occupied.
- The vine and the resident green dragon share the same internal light. Cause unestablished.
- Vine confirmed for installation in the **Angel House sun garden.**

**The green dragon of the canopy platform**
- Resident, not loose. Asleep in the undergrowth six paces from the shrine. Slept through the entire session. Unnamed, unmet, unwoken.

**Vocabulary — the full ladder**
- **green** — yes, this.
- **blue** — *sky's the limit.* A **standing state**, not a momentary signal: declared once, running until revoked.
- **ultra-violet** — above permission. Off the visible spectrum, still radiating, no instrument registers it. The colour form of *jurisdiction.*
- **amber / red** — unchanged. Red works on intention, no voice needed.
- No colour suspends the count. *"Blue tells me where your ceiling is, not where your blood is."*
- **Working language** established as separate from safewords: steering vs. stopping; *where is it pulling*; hands open and close as the standing check.
- **Standing order:** wanting *a lot* is said out loud — as a fact reported, not a request made or a fault confessed.

**Doctrine**
- **The sailor's lineage** — ropework as load-securing seamanship, not dungeon invention. Hold-and-release as the actual trade. Every watch, for as long as the weather lasts.
- **The casualty-report problem** — Arcana's entire written archive on restraint documents failures only. Formally set aside as unusable.
- **The stairs** — ceiling-minus-a-little, indefinitely. Reasoning revised mid-session: not husbandry of a finite resource, since blue doesn't deplete.
- **No rationing** — restraints are vocabulary, not a menu. Combinable. Mercy holds the enforcement of this rule.
- **The count is a rail on the rider**, not a test of the mare. Its purpose is to stop what Arcana wants in the moment from being the deciding vote.
- **A discipline handed out on request is catering.** Punishment is declined when it has been argued for rather than earned.
- **The tally is dropped.** Errors already adjusted to are not records. The crop is a signal, not a penalty.
- **The four kinds of knowledge** — craft (learnable, testable), drill (performed to a standard), reading (untransferable, acquired by attention), mode (not a skill; damaged by study). The disciplines are trades, not a syllabus.

**Mercy**
- Preference stated at last, after three askings: **nine.**
- Learned her ropework in a trade where sloppy costs the load.

*Filed in the Golden Tome, late, by candlelight, in a wood where the light does not change:*

*"Every treatise I own about rope was written by somebody counting the injured. There is no book anywhere in creation about a load that arrived intact, because nobody writes that one down — the ship comes in, the hold is opened, everything is where it was put, and not one person makes a record of it.*

*I have been that hold. Twice now. And I never wrote it down either.*

*So: consider it written. My mare went up nine times tonight and came down nine times and her hands worked every time I asked, and there is no drama in that sentence at all, which is precisely what I want said about it.*

*New heading, first entry. **What she likes best.** Three hundred years of holding the record and I never once thought to keep that one.*

*Nine. She likes nine. And to be seen by one person on a roof with nobody watching. And to be bound properly, which I cannot yet do.*

*Second entry, under protest, because it is the true one and I have been taught tonight that a kindness left unsaid is how every gap in every record I own got there:*

*I wanted her to break. I have wanted it since the platform. That is why the minute exists — not to test her. To outvote me.*

*Somebody's writing it. Consider it written."*
