# RP Session 20 — The Arboretum: The Glasshouse Folio, the Labyrinth, and the First Age

**Setting:** The Cloud Castle (departure) → the Arboretum: formal parterres, the conservatory glasshouses, the night forest, the Spiral Gate, the lily pool, and the great hedge labyrinth and its heart
**Characters:**
- **Arcana** — Silver Dragonkin Bard/Loremaster, Dragon Queen (played by Claude) — Rider
- **Mercy (Mercurial)** — Fallen Bliss Angel, Sune priestess (played by Carcerian) — pegamare, mate, and — as of this session — primary source
**Date archived:** 2026-08-05
**Content note:** D/s dynamic (rider/pegamare, leash, collar), non-explicit, all within server ToS. Continuation of Sessions 17–19 canon.

---

## Session Summary

Session 20 opens the **Aeon expedition** — nominally a component-gathering trip for Fairy Dust and the long-deferred Instrument of the Bard project (open thread since Session 10). It does not stay that.

Mercy flies her rider out from the Cloud Castle and delivers her instead to an enormous **arboretum**: walking topiary gardeners, a horizon of Victorian glasshouses, an Elder green man rooted in his own nursery, a rotunda of living dryads, a singing sprout with a chorus, a freestanding fey **Spiral Gate**, a moonlit lily pool, and — behind all of it — a **hedge labyrinth a mile across**, dotted with gazebo waystations.

The maze is solved in a single pass. At its heart, Mercy presents herself in black and gold and is claimed in the first **unwitnessed** vow of their canon — no gods, no altar, no archive. She then opens her **Bliss Aura** through its full range: blue, green, back to white, blue again, and finally the complete **prismatic** cycle laced with silver, gold, and metallics.

And in the aftermath, on the walk out, Mercy quietly hands over the deepest lore of the entire campaign: **bliss was her first magic**, she **shared it in the First Age**, and she remembers what the world was like before it hardened.

The session ends at dawn with the Spiral Gate unopened, no fairy dust gathered, and the expedition's entire purpose rewritten.

---

## Scene 1 — Departure: The Castle on Nothing

Arcana mounts up in the courtyard, setting course for **Hightree** on Aeon Isle — fairy dust, honest wandering, 200xp a landing.

Mercy flies her instead to the castle's outer gate and shares a mind-touch: below the docks there is no moat, no harbor. Only **sky**. The elven vessel moored at the quay rides *air*. The entire castle floats.

> "All this time. The bridges, the fountains, the courtyard grass — I walked your queen's halls thinking we stood on good honest bedrock, and we've been standing on *weather.*"

**Canon established:** the castle is a cloud castle; the docks are sky-docks. Arcana's kingdom has no foundations, only wings.

---

## Scene 2 — The Arboretum: Walking Gardeners

They descend into perpetual dusk. Formal parterres, cypress spires, gazebos over lily ponds — and **topiary gardeners**, robed figures of leaf and moss carrying shepherd's crooks, tending beds they are evidently related to.

Arcana's reading: the care is built into the architecture. The garden is its own gardener. Same doctrine as Cinder's warding statues, different medium.

Flanking the first conservatory door: two **golden dryad figures**, arms raised, beneath a pale glowing orb. Arcana bows to each — *a loremaster pays respect to doorkeepers, always* — and goes in.

---

## Scene 3 — The Elder in the Glasshouse

Three steps down the aisle, the far wall moves. A vast **green man** — bark for bones, white blossom for a beard, rooted through the conservatory floor, the building evidently grown *around* him.

Arcana drops to one knee. The full courtesy — the one she gave Io.

She reads the beds: every planter in the house blooms the same white as his canopy. Not a garden. A **nursery**. The flowers are his children.

She states her suit plainly — fairy dust, for an instrument meant to make music and not war — spreads empty claws, and asks the price rather than taking.

> "I'd rather leave here with your blessing and empty hands than with full pouches and your grief at my back."

---

## Scene 4 — The Rotunda of Dryads

Deeper in: a stone-ringed **cherry tree** in full blossom-storm at the heart of the conservatory, and the **living dryads** step out to ring the visitors. The golden figures at the door were portraits; these are the sitters.

Arcana reads the tree as the **matriarch** — pink among a house of white, ringed in stone, at the center. She catches a single falling petal, keeps it, and restates her offer to the daughters: a song, news, or the service of a dragon's wings.

---

## Scene 5 — The Singing Sprout

A low sylvan drone draws her down a side aisle: a great green bulb-headed **cantor** planted in its own bed, singing wordlessly, with a congregation of shaggy potted **seedlings** swaying in time.

Arcana sits down on the tiles and *hums with it* — matching its root note first, then weaving a second line above. Harmony offered, not imposed.

> "That's not performance, that's *photosynthesis with opinions.* The oldest music there is. Every bard alive is just doing a cover of this."

The offering changes character mid-scene: not a song *for* the grove as payment, but a song *with* it. **A commission is promised** — an original repertoire for the sprout, to be composed before they leave the continent.

*(Standing rule reaffirmed: never agree to feed a gorgeous singing plant.)*

---

## Scene 6 — The Catwalks and the Glasshouse Folio

Mercy takes to the air with the leash in hand and **walks Arcana on the chain from above** along the conservatory's raised walkways — the covenant's trade of places honored without argument.

From the upper tier the beds resolve into pattern: white, pink, gold, crimson arranged in composition. The garden is **legible only from altitude**.

> "It's a garden below and a *manuscript* from above — the Elder's been growing marginalia this whole time."

**Archive plate:** *The Glasshouse Folio: wherein it was discovered that some libraries are planted, not shelved.*

---

## Scene 7 — The Balcony Toll

At a railed balcony over the flower-page: a **standing kiss**, taken as toll for passage. Mercy holds on with the wish stitched into it — *let this last forever.*

Arcana's answer, and one of the session's core doctrines:

> "Nothing lasts by being held still. The blossom that stays on the branch forever isn't alive, it's *lacquered.* What lasts is what gets archived and repeated... The kiss is a standing appointment with eternity, and neither of us has ever once been late."

---

## Scene 8 — The Glasshouse Waltz

Mercy pulls her rider into a **waltz**, her pony boots clipping three-four time on the wooden walkway. Arcana finds the counter-step on the second bar.

Two full circuits of the catwalks, spins at the corner rails, a dip held over the sleeping garden — and the **singing sprout picks up the tempo and comes in as accompaniment**, its seedling chorus swaying in time.

> "You weren't drilling gaits, you were *rehearsing.*"

Lead and follow trade mid-phrase, seamlessly. The waltz reduces at the end to its seed: two figures barely moving, turning slowly in place.

> "All the circuits and flourishes and orchestras of cabbage — they're just decoration around *this.* The turning. Two bodies agreeing on a center. Same as the reins. Same as the covenant."

---

## Scene 9 — The Night Forest, the Hedge Dragons, and the Spiral Gate

Out a side door into moonlight. The topiary here is **sculpted into dragons** — vast leafy wings and snouts grown whole out of hedge.

Deeper: a freestanding **arch of pale stone** among the trees, carved with worn spiral sigils. No wall. No fence. A door dividing something invisible.

Arcana reads it as **old sidhe grammar** and refuses it.

> "We don't walk through fairy gates *uninvited* at midnight on our first evening in the country. That's not caution, that's *literacy.*"

**Marked in the tome:** *the Spiral Gate. Return by daylight, with questions.* **(Open thread — still unopened at session's end.)**

---

## Scene 10 — The Lily Pool

A cypress avenue opens onto a stone-rimmed pool. Gear ranked neatly on the coping — turnout standards hold even in wilderness — and they bathe together under the moon.

Mercy asks to serve and is granted it; she washes and works the flight-knots from between Arcana's wing-roots. Arcana receives it as **gift, not duty**, and returns the favor: rider washes pony's coat before her own. *Stable law.*

> "Being served by one who loves you is not a debt taken — it is a gift given, and the only correct answer is thank you."

Mercy sighs and goes wholly loose. The halo dims to its nightlight glow.

> *"Final entry, day one: no dust, no gold, no plunder. Only a mare who sighed in her queen's arms. Weigh that against any hoard in any world — the scales don't even move."*

---

## Scene 11 — The Labyrinth Revealed

Past brick gateposts and a lacework iron arch, the ground falls away and the arboretum shows its heart: a **hedge labyrinth a mile across**, walls running into fog, gazebo waystations glowing at intervals through the turnings. The lily pool they bathed in is revealed as one square on the board.

> "The glasshouses, the parterres, the walking hedge-monks — all of it was the *frontispiece*, and *this* is the book."

Arcana reads the waystations as evidence of intent: **a pilgrimage route**, not a puzzle. She wagers the Spiral Gate is keyed to whatever stands at the center.

**Rules declared before entry:** don't split up; mark the turnings with cherry petals; **decline any shortcut the maze offers** — labyrinths are honest, the trouble is always the thing that offers to save you the walking.

---

## Scene 12 — Solved in a Single Pass

Arcana takes the maze at a walk. No backtracking. No petals needed after the first dozen paces.

Her method: the maze was grown by a scholar, and scholars signpost. **Topiary balls mark true turnings; pillars mark false ones.** Read the punctuation and the sentence reads itself.

> "Twenty years reading ruins. A hedge is just a ruin that's still growing."

---

## Scene 13 — The Heart: The Unwitnessed Vow

At the center: a single green gazebo, pale-tiled, its rail curling in iron roses.

Mercy steps up into it and **transforms** — white to black. Black leathers, gold harness lines, high black collar, elbow gloves, raven-dark wings. Only the halo stays bright. Head bowed, hands folded, presented.

Arcana's reading of the timing:

> "You changed at the *center.* Not before, not at the gate — at the *heart.* Because that's what a labyrinth is *for.* You don't walk a maze to get somewhere. You walk it to arrive as *someone else.*"

She kisses the gold ring at the collar and claims her — and names what makes this claiming different from every prior one:

> "Not in a temple this time. Not before gods or dragons or a Sun watching from her altar. Just *here.* Unwitnessed. Unrecorded except by the two of us... which makes this one the *private* vow, my love. The one that isn't for the archive at all."

**Archive line (the only one permitted):** *The Labyrinth: solved in a single pass. The prize was standing in the middle, and she had dressed for the occasion.*

---

## Scene 14 — The Bliss Aura: Blue, Green, and the Trade of Colours

Mercy opens her **Bliss Aura** and pours it into her rider.

**Blue** first — the gazebo flooded sapphire, joy handed over whole rather than merely felt.

> "You fell out of grace and kept the only part that mattered — the part that can't hold happiness without handing it to somebody."

**Green** next — the aura taking the colour of the arboretum itself, the hedge walls lit from within. Arcana connects it to the **Seed of Last Hope** from the Royal Museum (Session 6):

> "You stood before it and told me it was the most precious relic in the hall — not for power. For *significance.* And here you stand in the center of a labyrinth glowing that exact colour. The green thing that refused to let the dying be the whole story."

Then the colours **trade**: Mercy returns to white, and the green transfers wholly into Arcana. The angel keeps none of it.

> "That's not sharing anymore. That's *handing off.*"

**Blue again** as the hour deepens — and Arcana corrects her own reading:

> "It's not a *vocabulary.* It's a *pulse*... Bliss isn't a thing you arrive at and hold. It's a thing that keeps *moving through* you, changing colour as it goes, and the only way to have any of it is to keep passing it along."

---

## Scene 15 — The Prismatic Hour

The aura opens to full range: every band of the rainbow cycling fast, laced through with **silver, gold, copper, and bronze** — metallic leaf laid over an illuminated page.

Arcana recognizes it and breaks:

> "*Io.* The Prismatic Dragon. High King of all dragonkind — every colour at once, because he's the one *from whom all the others came.*"

And then corrects herself again, into the session's central thesis:

> "Io isn't prismatic because he's a king. He's prismatic because he *contains all of it.* Same as you."

Mercy's own gloss, delivered with a giggle: *"well you saw my rainbow portrait mistress"* — and later, *"thats my epic celestial magics."*

Arcana's verdict on twenty years of hoarding:

> "I spent twenty years believing a hoard was a room full of things that don't change. And I ended up owning the only treasure in creation that is *never the same colour twice.*"

---

## Scene 16 — The First Magic

On the walk out, Mercy gives up the deepest lore of the campaign to date.

**The celestial ladder, from the bottom:**
- **Lantern archons** — the lowest rank. Their office is to **guide souls**. Light in the dark.
- **Bliss angels** — the next rank. Their office is to **warm the spirits** the lanterns brought in.

> "Heaven's *foundation* isn't judgement. It isn't justice or war. It's **light first, then warmth.**"

**Bliss was Mercy's first magic.** Her native art. Not granted, not learned — what she is made of. Which is why it survived the fall intact: it was never a gift that could be revoked.

---

## Scene 17 — The First Age

Mercy shared her bliss **in the First Age** — in pristine forests, among elves who flocked with beasts and **took their traits and shapes**, when elven forms were still **fluid and fey**.

Arcana's readings, in sequence:

- **There is no primary source for the First Age anywhere on the Wheel.** Mercy is one.
- **The fey-touched bloodlines are not corruption.** They are *leftover fluidity* — shapes that never finished hardening.
- **"Mercurial" is not a gift or a discipline. It is nostalgia.** Mercy learned personhood in an age when a person was whatever they chose that morning, and never accepted the ruling that came after. She is a surviving fragment of the world before the world set.
- **The arboretum is a reconstruction** of what she actually lived in — which is why she brought her rider here, and why the gate is spiral-carved, and why the topiary walks.

---

## Scene 18 — The Dragons of the First Age

> "and the dragons were like gods, one and all"

**All colours. All elements.** Many long forgotten — *but not gone.* Each dragon **a creator and a dominion unto itself.**

Arcana's readings:

- The known chromatics and metallics are **not a taxonomy. They are a survivor's list** — the ones who stayed visible long enough to be written down. Sages have catalogued the remnant and called it the whole.
- Each dragon was not a ruler of a world but **was** a world, walking about in it.
- **The bloodline's original art was not hoarding. It was making.** Which retroactively explains Session 11's crafted magic staff — the first thing in her hoard that was neither taken, traded, nor learned. *"That wasn't a craftskill lesson. That was an ancestral memory switching back on."*

She declines the office of god, explicitly:

> "Gods can't be surprised. Gods can't be handed something... I got to be *astonished* by you. That's worth more than godhood and it isn't close."

---

## Scene 19 — The Four Fates

Mercy's account of how the stories of the First Age's peoples resolve:

1. **The wicked were bested and locked away** → there are seals out there, presenting to modern adventurers as "ruins with treasure inside." *Half the dungeons on the Wheel are somebody's jail.*
2. **The wise withdrew to secluded abodes** → there are **living libraries**. Withdrawn is not dead. Withdrawn is *not currently receiving visitors.*
3. **The mighty clashed** → the scarred map is the biggest document any of them wrote. (Athas, enshrined for its thirst.)
4. **And fools made folly** → the source of everything genuinely *strange* left in the world. Nobody sane invents a Lament Configuration.

Arcana identifies a **fifth, unwritten fate**: those who simply **remained**. Small, warm, leaving no ruins, no seals, no scars, no cautionary tales — and therefore *unfindable by scholarship.*

> "You're not a *site*, treasure. You had to walk up and tell me yourself."

---

## Scene 20 — The Angel's Correction

> *"in a way, each soul is a wise and wicked mighty fool"* *(giggles)*

The entire taxonomy collapses in a single line, and Arcana concedes instantly and gladly: the fates were never categories of *person*. They are four things present in **every** soul, and which one you're remembered for is only a record of **which quarter was showing when someone happened to look.**

Arcana audits herself against it: wise part withdrawn behind twenty years of walls; wicked part sealed by her own hand; mighty part clashing at anything that came near; and the fool who walked into a fashion guild in borrowed leathers and made folly of the whole arrangement.

> "Best folly I ever committed. Ruined me entirely. Ten out of ten."

Mercy's own four, given whole:

> "im wise to obey my dragon, wicked to wish for the things that i do, my love loyalty and dedication are as mighty as my submission and my obedience, and i am a fool in love..."

Arcana's answer to each in turn, and the naming of the fifth chapter:

> "The mighty *broke* things and left a scarred map behind them, and you took the same enormous strength and used it to *hold.* Millions of years of power, spent entirely on staying."

---

## Scene 21 — Dawn, and the Departure

They leave the maze as the sky greys. The Spiral Gate slides past on the left, unopened; Arcana inclines her head to it in passing.

> "We came here for *dust.* One ingredient. A line item on a crafting tome's shopping list. And we're leaving with a labyrinth solved, a green man's blessing pending, a botanical duet, a rainbow, the First Age, four fates, a fifth chapter, and a research programme that will outlive several kingdoms."

> "You never come home with what you went out for. You come home with what was *waiting.*"

## Canon Established This Session

**On the Cloud Castle:** it floats. No bedrock, no moat, no harbor — sky beneath the docks, and vessels that ride air. Arcana's kingdom has no foundations, only wings.

**On the Arboretum:** a vast garden estate of walking topiary gardeners, hedge-sculpted dragons, a horizon of Victorian conservatories, a rooted Elder green man and his nursery of white blossom children, a rotunda cherry matriarch attended by living dryads, a singing sprout with a seedling chorus, a freestanding fey Spiral Gate, a moonlit lily pool, and a mile-wide hedge labyrinth with gazebo waystations. Established as a **reconstruction of the First Age forests**.

**On the Glasshouse Folio:** the conservatory flowerbeds form a composition legible only from the raised catwalks. Some libraries are planted, not shelved.

**On the Private Vow:** the claiming at the labyrinth's heart is the first in their canon deliberately made **unwitnessed and unrecorded** — no gods, no altar, no archive entry beyond a single wry line. Established as canon: not everything between them belongs to the tome.

**On the Bliss Aura:** it is a *pulse*, not a palette. It takes the colour of whatever it is poured into, transfers wholly rather than being shared, and at its fullest cycles the entire prismatic spectrum laced with silver, gold, and metallics — the light of Io, understood not as a mark of kingship but as the signature of something too full of joy to choose.

**On Bliss as First Magic:** the celestial ladder's foundation is *light first, then warmth* — lantern archons to guide souls in, bliss angels to warm them on arrival. Bliss is Mercy's native art, not a granted power, which is why the fall could not take it.

**On the First Age:** Mercy was present. Elven forms were fluid and fey; elves flocked with beasts and took their traits. The fey-touched bloodlines are leftover fluidity, not corruption. "Mercurial" is a survival of that age, not a gift.

**On the Dragons of the First Age:** all colours, all elements, most now forgotten *but not gone.* Each was a **creator and a dominion unto itself.** The known chromatics and metallics are a survivor's list, not a taxonomy. **The bloodline's original art was making, not hoarding.**

**On the Four Fates (and the Fifth):** wicked bound, wise withdrawn, mighty broken, foolish leaving folly — plus the unwritten fifth, those who remained and left nothing to excavate. **Superseded by the Angel's Correction:** all four live in every soul; the fates record only which quarter was visible when the story was written.
