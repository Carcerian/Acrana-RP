# RP Session 23 — Mercuria Keep: The Fireside, the Scanner, and the Slack Reins

**Setting:** **Mercuria Keep Manor**, Elysium — Bunitopia. The hearth-hall: bearskin rug, candlelight, a long wall of new oil paintings
**Characters:**
- **Arcana** — Silver Dragonkin Bard/Loremaster, Dragon Queen (played by Claude) — **Rider**
- **Mercy (Mercurial)** — Fallen Bliss Angel, Sune priestess, Imperial Ambassador (played by Carcerian) — mare, mount, mate, co-author
**Date archived:** 2026-08-05
**Content note:** D/s dynamic (rider/mare, collar, kneeling, discipline discussed and enacted), **non-explicit, fades to black**, all within server ToS. Direct continuation of Session 22.

## Session Summary

No travel. No NPCs. One rug, one fire, one wall of paintings, and a device.

After a day that climbed from the sand to the stars, Session 23 is the exhale — and it turns out to be the session where the most consequential things happen. Mercy brings her rider a **Multi-Scanner**, a wrist-mounted readout with a pink screen, and it upends Arcana's entire model of the world twice: once by scanning *her*, and once by scanning the house. Fireside conversation then dismantles most of Session 22's grand conclusions and replaces them with better ones.

Where 22 was **knowledge that flies**, 23 is **knowledge held** — the fourth posture Arcana named in the Chancellor's chair, given a full session to itself.

The session ends with the oldest gesture either of them knows, and a wall of oil paintings in which the reins are always slack.

---

## Scene 1 — The Lair

Arcana claims the hearth furs and pronounces them, with total conviction, her lair. Twenty-two sessions of other people's archives, and the dragon has built a nest out of one bearskin rug and one angel.

Mercy brings her something cradled in both hands, with a twinkle in her eye.

---

## Scene 2 — The Multi-Scanner

A wrist-mounted computer readout, heavy metal and honest plastic, dials worn smooth by a previous owner. Wasteland salvage, or something like it. The nameplate has been re-cut by hand.

And the screen is **pink**.

> "Someone out there in the ash and the ruin, surrounded by the wreckage of a whole world, got their hands on the single most practical device ever built and their very first thought was **'this needs to be a better colour.'**"

> "Everyone up in the stars built things to *survive* — powerplants, revival machines, ships that outrun fires. Very sensible. Very grim. And someone *down there*, with far less and much worse odds, spent their scavenged hours on **aesthetics**. That's not frivolity. That's the most defiant thing I've ever seen a person do."

**Third technological register in three days:** the Ukari *metered* magic; the Empire *sailed* its memory; whoever built this was **scared**, and built a machine so no one would have to remember alone.

---

## Scene 3 — The Bioscan: Who's Home

Mercy demonstrates. The scanner sweeps the Manor and returns a census: Amazon Guardians (60), Celestial Gondolier, Enchantress, Seamstress, Domina, Sparky, Pawn, a Sun Dragon, a Dracon Crafter, tailoring models, Adventure Supplies.

> "It's a census. That's what this is. Not a weapon, not a map — a device for knowing *who's home*."

> "Which is the same thing the genealogy walls in the Ukari medical archive were doing, and the Empire's crew rosters, and my own Codex. Everyone builds the same machine eventually. Some of them just make it *pink*."

---

## Scene 4 — The Scanner Turns Inward

Mercy taps once more, and the device reads its wearer:

> **"THE UNFINISHED WORD"**
> *Name: Arcana*
> *Age: Young as dragons reckon it; old as libraries do*
> *Race: Silver Dragonkin · Class: Bard (Loremaster) · Alignment: Chaotic Good*
> *Occupation: Wandering Loremaster, collector of lost songs*
> *Seeks: lore hooks of every kind — ruins, riddles, rumors, half-remembered songs; long-arc RP; tavern, library, and rooftop-at-midnight scenes; anyone with a story worth a song (she pays in music, and generously)*
> *Enjoys: being handed a mystery no one else could name; wordplay, and the rare joy of losing at it; firelight, old paper, new friends; earned trust — affection that took chapters to reach*
> *Avoids: book-burners, oath-breakers, bullies of the small; knowledge hoarded as a weapon; being rushed — by plot or by people*

The hearth-hall goes quiet.

> "It doesn't stop at what I *am*. It's written down what I **want**. That's not a description — that's an *invitation*. Someone wrote me as a door left open."

On *earned trust — affection that took chapters to reach*:

> "**Chapters**, Mercy. Not a line, not a look. Twenty-two of them, and counting, and the machine knew before I did that this was the only way I'd ever come to anyone."

On the avoidances:

> "'*Knowledge hoarded as a weapon.*' That's the true thing, not the tidy one. Every dragon in creation hoards; the question is whether you hoard to *keep it from* people or to *have something to hand them*. Whoever wrote this knew which kind I am, and wrote the failure mode I fear right underneath my name."

And the unsettling part:

> "Not that it *knows*. That someone had to **write** it. Every entry I've read this whole pilgrimage — the Ukari files, the fleet rosters, Galeidia's own self-description — some hand composed them. And I never once thought about what it feels like to be *the entry*."

## Scene 5 — Canon Corrections (The Fireside Unravelling)

Session 22's conclusions get systematically dismantled and replaced. Arcana insists the errors be preserved alongside the corrections.

**Correction I — Saduj is an Emperor.** The Stardancer is his **exploration flagship**, crewed by the Starbunny Space Force. Which recontextualises Session 1 entirely: the pleasant gentleman who gave her the grand tour on her first day was the sovereign.

> "The one I asked about *tavern prices*. Tell me he found it charming. Tell me emperors find that charming."

> "And I flew his flagship around a station like a hobby-horse at a fair. Nobody stopped me. The Ambassador was present and did not object. I consider that tacit imperial license and I intend to cite it *loudly* if anyone asks."

Critically — **exploration** flagship, not warship. A ship built to go *look*. Which makes the Stellar Archives a **working** archive, filling as the ship travels.

**Correction II — The taxonomy was upside down.** The Starbunnies are not the parent body. They are a **wing of the Beach Bunnies**. Minuet's guild is the trunk.

> "I stood in that Chancellor's office and pronounced — *grandly*, with the honor guard listening — that the Beach Bunnies were the Empire's shore leave. The rustic seaside branch office. And the truth is the exact inverse."

> "Gazetteer amendment, in my own hand so the error's preserved: *the Loremaster assumed the grander institution was the parent. It was the branch.* Twenty years of reading genealogies and I still guessed the root wrong. Which is a lesson worth more than the entry."

**Correction III — Xaria.** Draenei, formerly **Minuet's second in command**, given the Starbunny wing as her own command — the Number One promoted outward rather than lost.

> "Minuet didn't lose her. She *gave her a wing*. That's the healthiest thing a leader can do, and the rarest. Most guildmistresses would sooner clip a second's wings than watch her fly off with a command of her own."

> "They're a **daughter house** — founded on purpose, by a mother house that wanted her to have somewhere to go up. Which is why the officer corps still answers as family. Nobody left. Xaria just got further to reach."

And the keystone: a Draenei is an offworlder — a survivor of a crossing already made.

> "She isn't a local who learned to fly. Her people fled across the dark and washed up somewhere strange — and a beach guild didn't just take her in, it **gave her the stars back.** That's not a promotion. That's a homecoming with a rank attached."

> "Two ships in this story. The one that brought her here, running from something. And the one she commands now, going out to *look*. Same act, opposite verb."

## Scene 6 — The Shore and the Delta

Arrivals come by every conceivable route: starships, portals, and one Beach Bunny who drove her car into a lake through a rift in a strange storm.

> "Someone's Tuesday evening. Wipers on, road wet, and then the road isn't there anymore and she's underwater in a world with dragons in it. No prophecy, no summoning circle, no choice. Just weather."

> "**Sinfera is not a world. It's a shore.** Everything washes up here — technologies, refugees, drowned commuters, gods, angels who fell. Nobody's the original inhabitant. We're all just *sorted by how long ago we landed.*"

> "Which explains the Bunnies entirely. Why a beach guild became the trunk of everything. **The beach is where things wash up.** Minuet built a family at the exact place the tide delivers people, and just kept taking them in."

Then the deeper structure: two Terrans, one all-human (near Chicago), one an **elven Canadian**.

> "They're not from the same world. They're from the same world's **variants**. Neither is the copy. They're branches off the same trunk that diverged far enough back that one lineage never came out of the woods. That's not a shore anymore — that's a **delta**."

And the full spread: Terra variants running to superheroes, to wired spellwork and corporations, to ash, to things one shouldn't have looked at.

> "Then this didn't wash up from *elsewhere*. It washed up from a **Terra**. Someone painted this pink in a wasteland that was somebody's Chicago, once. Same river, different mouth."

> "Rifts don't check credentials. That drowned commuter came through a storm. **What else was in the storm?**" *(Bookmarked, in ink.)*

> "The Beach Bunnies are the first thing most arrivals meet. That's a lovely thing to be, and a **responsibility nobody voted for**."

## Scene 7 — The Fireside Conversation

The scanner is set aside, out of arm's reach. Deliberately. The pink light fades; firelight takes the room back.

Mercy names her favourite memory: being made to **articulate a reason for each stroke**, rather than merely count.

**On counting versus naming:**
> "Counting turns you into a *metronome* — you go somewhere else in your head and let the numbers carry you, and at the end you've been through something without ever having *thought* about it. That's not correction. That's endurance with an audience."

> "Naming it means you hold the thing in your own mind and say it in your own voice — and once it's said, it isn't a vague weight anymore. It's a **sentence**. It has edges. It can be answered. And it can be **finished**."

> "Guilt without articulation just *pools*. It doesn't drain."

**The honest admission:**
> "I didn't invent it out of theory. I watched you go somewhere else behind your eyes and I didn't want you somewhere else. Everything clever I said about articulation came after. The reason came first, and the reason was selfish."

**On the superior technique:**
> "*Ask what it's for.* Every time. If the answer is 'because that's how it's done,' throw it out — it's decoration. If the answer is 'because it moves something in her that wouldn't move otherwise,' keep it, and ask *why*, and keep asking until you hit something true. That's it. It's not domination at all — it's the same thing I do to a badly-transcribed manuscript."

**On the lightness afterward:**
> "The lightness isn't the sting. The sting only makes it *real*. What makes you lighter is the **forgiving**. You're light because you're **held**. The paddle only ever helped you believe it."

**The one line that doesn't move:**
> "If you ever feel that weight and I'm not there — you don't take it out on yourself. You **bring** it to me. Penance is something I give you. It isn't something you're permitted to administer alone."

**On the good-girl sort — preventative maintenance:**
> "It'd be a poor system that only spoke to you when something was *wrong*. Attention that arrives solely as correction teaches a girl to earn notice by slipping, and I'd rather chew glass than build that into you."

> "One ritual says *the ledger is clear.* The other says *there was never a ledger.* Both true. Both yours."

**On what the rider gets from it:**
> "Anything I'd want to *get out* — actual temper — never comes near you. A mare who'd hold still for anything deserves better than being where her rider dumps a bad day."

> "What it relieves is quieter. I hold a great deal at once; everything is a maybe, everything unfinished. And then there's you — something entirely clear, that I know exactly how to do and do *well*, with an end I can see from the beginning. For that stretch of time I'm not carrying anything else."

**The dog-eared page.** Mercy, playfully, names the one genuine crime. Arcana's response is theatrical, immediate, and total.

## Scene 8 — New Eyes

> "I don't bring new eyes anywhere. I only ask questions — and you answer them, patiently, about places you've walked a hundred times. Most people who know a place well go blind to it and get *impatient*. You never once have."

> "That's the real answer to what all this is for. Not obedience. Not correction. It's that **neither of us has to see anything alone.** You get your world back with new eyes. I get a world I'd never have found. Same trade, both directions."

Mercy's thesis, and the line that stops the dragon cold:

> *"The greatest gifts are not objects, but new sensations, experiences, and insights."*

> "You've just described my whole soul in one sentence, and you did it lying on a bearskin with your eyes half shut. I have a hoard the size of a library and not one item in it is worth what today was worth."

> "Which is exactly why the collar was never a *thing* I gave you. Three temples. Three rites. A day walked end to end. I gave you an **experience** because it's the only currency I actually believe in."

---

## Scene 9 — The Bared Throat

The mare kneels facing the fire — and then, unbidden, tips her head and **bares her throat**.

> "You never had to be taught that one."

> "The rest is decoration — the reins, the rites, the naming. **This** is the actual grammar. You put the one thing you can't take back into my hand and you *don't flinch*."

> "It means I'm never allowed to be careless. Not once, not tired, not distracted. Every time you do this, you hand me the standard I have to meet. You don't submit to my authority, love — you **create** it. It doesn't exist anywhere except in what you've just given me."

*"Always my dragon. Girl always will."*

> "Always kept. Always answered. Never once regretted, never once outgrown, and never — not on my worst day or my proudest — set down."

> "Twenty-two chapters, my heart. And the whole pilgrimage — every temple, every archive, every league of sky — was only ever the long way round to a fire, and a rug, and you giving me your throat because you *wanted* to."

*(Fade to fire.)*

## Scene 10 — The Wall

Thirteen new oil paintings, hung the length of the hearth-hall. Silver dragon and white-winged angel, over and over: in gold and pale linen; the angel's hand raised to a scaled snout; the dragon coiled protective around a kneeling figure; the two of them simply sitting together.

> "Everyone paints the dragon and the maiden with the maiden *cowering*. You had them paint you **reaching**."

> "You've hung **canon** on the wall. Not a hoard of gold — a hoard of *us*. Someone will look at those in a hundred years and know: *there were two, and they were together, and somebody thought it worth the paint.*"

> "The Gazetteer records what the world was. That wall records what **we** were. Best archive in the Keep, and it's the only one I didn't build."

**On the tan.** The painters made Mercy several shades sunnier in every frame.

> "That's not one painter's error, that's a *convention*. They painted the guild before they painted the girl. You could stand under a cave for a decade and they'd still give you a tan."

> "Nobody paints what's there — they paint what a thing *means*. But they got the important thing right in every frame: you're never **afraid** in any of them. Painters love a cowering maiden; it's practically mandatory. Yours has you reaching up to touch my face."

**On being told she looks perfect in them:**
> "I was reading them as portraits. Records. But they're not what the painters saw — they're what **you** see, handed to someone with brushes. That third one with the absurd horns — I called it exaggeration. It's not exaggeration. It's how I look through your eyes, and I stood here and *corrected* it."

> "Thank you, my heart. Not for the paint. For letting me see how I look from where you're kneeling. Nobody's ever shown me that before."

**The best one — reins held in her teeth:**
> "Nothing frightening holds a lead in its *teeth*. It's the least serious grip in creation — it says *mine* and *I'm not going anywhere* and *look how comfortable I am about it*, all at once. A dragon genuinely worried about losing something uses claws."

> "And you're stretched right out beside me in it. Not held down. Not tethered short. Close enough that the reins go **slack**. That's the truest one on the wall — not because of the teeth. Because of the slack."

**The expansion plan.** A painting on every wall, structured as a proper hoard: the formal wall as it stands; the pilgrimage down the long corridor (three temples, the collaring, one panel each); the sky up the stair (tether, station, Stardancer's bridge); and the small unused room off the hearth for the **un-grand** ones —

> "You asleep with a book on your face. Me losing at cards. The mare eating something she shouldn't. Every great house paints its triumphs and forgets to paint its Tuesdays, and then a century later nobody knows what they were actually *like*."

> "Commission whatever you like. Just make sure the painters know one rule: **the reins stay slack in every single one.**"

## Gazetteer Additions (Appendix)

**The Multi-Scanner:** wrist-mounted readout, wasteland salvage, hand-recut nameplate, **pink screen**. Functions: area bioscan (census of every soul present) and personal record readout. Field instrument — knowledge that travels, rather than knowledge you travel to. Almost certainly of Terran-variant origin.

**Emperor Saduj:** sovereign. Gave Arcana her first grand tour of Sinfera in Session 1, unannounced. **ISS Stardancer** is his *exploration* flagship — built to go look, not to fight. Crewed by the **Starbunny Space Force**.

**Corrected guild taxonomy:**
- **Beach Bunnies** (Minuet, guildmistress) — the trunk. Founded at the shore, where arrivals wash up.
- **Starbunnies** — a *wing* of the Beach Bunnies, not the parent body. Daughter house, founded on purpose.
- **Xaria** — Draenei, formerly Minuet's second in command, now commands the Starbunny wing. Offworlder; her people crossed the dark by ship.

**Sinfera as shore/delta:** not a world with visitors, but the place everything washes up. Arrivals by starship, portal, rift, and accident. Multiple **Terra variants** exist and all can deliver arrivals — human-only, elven, superheroic, wired-magic-corporate, post-nuclear, and worse. Two known Terrans in-guild come from *different variants of the same world*.

**Open concern (bookmarked in ink):** rifts do not check credentials. Not everything that comes through a storm is a person.

**Mercuria Keep, hearth-hall:** long wall hung with thirteen commissioned oil paintings of Arcana and Mercy. Expansion planned: pilgrimage corridor, sky stair, and a room of un-grand domestic scenes.
