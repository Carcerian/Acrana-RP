# RP Session 7 — Fireworks, Family, and the Lady of the Isle
*Ambrosia Beach & Parrot Isle — July 5, 2026*
*Present: Arcana; Mercy/Mercurial; Silveny Laanilm; Sable Crowe-Baen'Und; Astraea Mercuria; Pirouette Baen'Und; Steven Seagull (Ambrosia Beach); Jonathan Livingston Seagull & the Phoenix (Parrot Isle)*

---

## SCENE 1 — Introductions Under Fireworks

*[Silveny Laanilm introduces herself: twin sister to Xanya, who is mother of Eleecil and grandmother of Myrna. Sable introduces Mercurial as her Godmother, Pirouette and Astraea as aunts.]*

**Arcana:** *settles onto the edge of the picnic blanket, folding her wings neatly behind her as another burst of colors blooms overhead, the light catching silver scales along her cheekbones*

Silveny Laanilm... *she repeats the name thoughtfully, as if filing it away in some vast internal library* Twin to Xanya, mother of Eleecil, grandmother of Myrna. Three generations in a single breath — that is a lineage worth remembering. *a warm smile* Names are the first verse of any story, and I do collect stories.

*she glances toward Sable, then to Mercurial, tilting her head with gentle curiosity*

And a Godmother besides. It seems I've wandered into a family reunion wearing a stranger's boots. *soft laughter* Fear not — bards make excellent guests at such gatherings. We trade songs for supper and gossip for wine.

*as a particularly large firework crackles gold across the sky, her eyes follow it upward, reflecting the light*

Whoever is conducting this display has a flair for timing. Gold, just as introductions finish — as if the sky itself approves of new acquaintances. *she looks back to Silveny* Tell me, do you and your sister share the twin's gift? I've known twins who finish each other's sentences across entire continents... and others who couldn't agree on the color of the sea.

## SCENE 2 — The Scout

**Arcana:** *remains standing at the blanket's edge beneath the red-and-white umbrella, one hand resting lightly on its pole as the sea breeze stirs her robes. A gull hops closer across the sand, eyeing the picnic with the shameless ambition of its kind*

*she lowers her voice conspiratorially, nodding toward the bird*

We are being scouted. *a flicker of amusement crosses her silver features* Brazen little creature — it has the eyes of a seasoned cutpurse. Guard your bread accordingly, ladies.

*another firework unfurls over the water, washing the beach in brief crimson light, and she tilts her head back to watch it fade, wings settling with a soft rustle*

A fine evening for it. Sand still warm from the day, sky putting on airs... *she glances back down at the gathered company with an easy smile, content to let the conversation drift where it will*

---

## SCENE 3 — Bard's College

*[Silveny: "I suppose I will find out, Mercy enrolled me in Bard's College" *she smiles*]*

**Arcana:** *her whole demeanor brightens at that, wings lifting slightly in unmistakable delight*

Bard's College! *she presses a hand to her chest in mock ceremony* Then we are to be colleagues, Silveny — I claim the privilege of being the first to welcome you to the trade.

*she crouches gracefully at the blanket's edge, closer to eye level now, silver eyes glinting with genuine enthusiasm*

And Mercy enrolled you, did she? *a fond glance toward Mercurial* She has an eye for potential — always has. Trust her judgment on this even when the scales and modes make your head swim in the first weeks. They will. Everyone's does.

*she counts off on elegant fingers, half teasing, half sincere*

Advice freely given, and worth every copper you paid for it: first, your instrument matters less than your ear — learn to *listen* before you learn to play. Second, the Lore halls are worth twice the time the performance halls are, though no one admits it. And third... *a conspiratorial smile* ...befriend the college cooks immediately. Bards sing for supper, but the wise ones sing for seconds.

*she straightens again, the firework light catching her scales*

What draws you to it — the music itself, or the stories it carries?

---

## SCENE 4 — The Proverb Comes Home

*[Silveny: "The college cook told me 'Bards sing for supper, but the wise ones sing for seconds.'"]*

**Arcana:** *goes utterly still for a heartbeat — then throws her head back and laughs, a bright, musical sound that startles the gull into a few indignant hops*

That old thief! *she wipes at her eye, wings shaking with mirth* I *coined* that line. Traded it to a college cook years ago for an extra ladle of stew and the secret to her honey bread — and now she serves it to new students as her own wisdom!

*she plants her hands on her hips, though the grin ruins any attempt at indignation*

Do you know what this means, Silveny? It means the line has outlived my telling of it. Passed cook to student, kitchen to kitchen... *her voice softens into something genuinely pleased* That is the truest thing I can teach you about our trade, better than any lecture in the Lore halls: a good line, once loosed, belongs to everyone and no one. You'll know you've made something worthwhile when strangers quote you back to yourself.

*she gives a small, theatrical bow toward the blanket*

Though I *will* be having words with that cook about royalties. A second ladle of stew per telling seems fair.

---

## SCENE 5 — Stew Economics & the Quiet Blessing

*[Silveny giggles; Astraea smirks.]*

**Arcana:** *catches Astraea's smirk and arches a silver brow in return, entirely unrepentant*

Ah, I see that look, Lady Astraea. You are thinking a dragonkin who haggles in ladles of stew has priced herself rather cheaply. *she smooths her robes with exaggerated dignity* But consider: gold spends once. A cook's favor compounds *daily*. It is the soundest investment in any college.

*she settles down onto the sand at last, near the blanket's edge, arranging her wings comfortably behind her as the surf hushes against the shore*

Besides — the honey bread recipe alone was worth a king's ransom. I have performed for three courts that fed me worse.

*a comfortable pause as she watches the last firework smoke drift apart over the water, then glances around at the gathered family with something warmer beneath the wit*

You keep fine company, Mercy. A beach, a blanket, three generations of names, and now a fledgling bard among them. *her tail curls loosely around her feet in the sand* Evenings like this are the ones the songs get written about — though no one ever believes that until years later.

## SCENE 6 — Everyone's a Critic

*[The seagull, unimpressed, stares at the party.]*

**Arcana:** *follows the collective gaze to the seagull, which stands rigid in the sand, fixing the entire party with one flat, disapproving eye*

*she leans toward it slightly, addressing it with complete gravity*

No? Nothing? *a beat* Everyone's a critic.

*she sighs and shakes her head, wings drooping in theatrical defeat*

Do you know, in all my years of performing, the harshest audiences have never been kings or archmages — it is *always* the wildlife. A cat once walked out of my finest ballad in Waterdeep. Simply stood, stretched, and left. Mid-crescendo.

*she plucks a small crust of bread from the picnic spread and tosses it a polite distance toward the gull, a bribe offered with the weary dignity of a performer who knows exactly what her audience wants*

There. Payment for your patience, honored critic. *dryly* Note that it took the bird thirty heartbeats of glaring to earn what the college cook charged me a proverb for. The gull drives the harder bargain.

*[A player echoes: looks to the seagull — "Everyone's a critic."]*

**Arcana:** *her head turns slowly at hearing her own words echoed back, and a grin spreads across her silver features — equal parts delight and vindication*

There! *she gestures triumphantly toward the speaker, then to the whole blanket* You all witnessed it. Not an hour ago I told Silveny: you know you've made something worthwhile when strangers quote you back to yourself. And here we are — the lesson demonstrating itself before the lecture has even gone cold.

*she inclines her head graciously, as though accepting an award*

The line is yours now. Spend it freely — it works on cats, critics, and disappointing weather as well.

*meanwhile the gull sidles toward the tossed crust with elaborate indifference, pretending to inspect the sand nearby, and Arcana watches it from the corner of her eye*

Oh, don't put on airs, sir. We have all seen you eyeing it. *she lowers her voice to the others, mock-solemn* Watch — it will wait until it believes no one is looking, snatch the prize, and fly off without so much as a review. Critics never tip.

## SCENE 7 — Lumie, and the Family Chart

*[Astraea: "I should ask Lumie-mom, she is a bard." "And grew up here." Pirouette: "Lumie is a friend of ours too." Clarified: Lumi is Mercy's daughter AND Astraea's mother.]*

**Arcana:** *pauses, recalculating the family constellation with the focused air of a scholar redrawing a lineage chart mid-lecture*

Wait — *Lumie-mom.* *she looks at Astraea with fresh understanding, then lets out a soft laugh at herself* Of course. Not merely a friend of the family — your *mother*. Forgive me; I heard 'bard' and my ears galloped ahead of my wits.

*she traces an idle line in the sand with one claw, as if sketching the branches*

So the coast raised a bard, the bard raised you, and now Silveny takes up the trade in turn... *she looks up, genuinely charmed* This family does not merely keep its stories — it breeds the tellers. Do you know how rare that is? Most lineages I've catalogued pass down swords, or grudges, or unfortunate noses. Yours passes down *music*.

*she inclines her head to Astraea, sincere beneath the levity*

Then I should like to meet your mother all the more. A daughter's introduction is worth ten letters of recommendation — and I confess I want to hear what songs a childhood on this beach puts into a person. If the gull features in any of them, I shall not be the least surprised.

## SCENE 8 — The Listening Kind of Love

*[Silveny smiles at Mercurial: "Silent enjoyer huh?" Mercurial: "mm just enjoying time with the extended family" *she snuggles Sable*]*

**Arcana:** *her gaze softens as she watches Mercy draw Sable close, and for once the bard lets a moment pass without filling it — the fireworks blooming vast and blue-gold over the water, painting the whole beach in shifting light*

*after a while, she answers Silveny's observation in a lower voice, as though reluctant to disturb the picture*

The quiet ones, Silveny, are usually the ones drinking it in deepest. *a small nod toward Mercy and Sable* Some people enjoy an evening by narrating it. Others simply... hold it. Godmother and godchild, blanket and firelight — she is not silent, truly. She is *listening*. There is a difference.

*Arcana folds her hands loosely before her, wings settling as another burst of light rolls its thunder gently across the waves*

A bard learns eventually that the finest evenings need no accompaniment. The sea keeps the rhythm, the sky provides the spectacle... *the faintest smile* ...and the family provides the reason. Everything else is ornamentation.

*[Sable snuggles into Mercy.]*

**Arcana:** *watches Sable nestle into Mercy's side, and something old and fond passes across her silver features — the expression of one who has catalogued a thousand grand histories and knows this small moment outweighs most of them*

*she speaks softly, mostly to herself, though loud enough for Silveny beside her*

There. That is the whole of it, really. Empires rise, towers fall, dragons hoard their gold and wonder why it never warms them... *a slight tilt of her head toward the pair* ...and the actual treasure is a blanket on the sand and someone who fits against your shoulder.

*the gull, crust long since claimed, has settled into the sand a respectful distance away, feathers fluffed against the night breeze — apparently having decided the company is tolerable after all*

*Arcana lowers herself fully to sitting, robes pooling around her, and gazes out at the dark water where the last firework smoke thins into stars*

I shall have to compose something for this evening. Nothing grand — grand would spoil it. A small song. Beach, blanket, three generations, one skeptical seagull. *a quiet smile* The kind you hum rather than perform.

## SCENE 9 — The Dragon Firework & the Ascending Angel

*[A vast dragon-shaped firework burns across the aurora; a player raises her camera for the perfect shot.]*

**Arcana:** *her head snaps up as the sky itself seems to catch fire — a vast, spined silhouette unfurling against the aurora, embers cascading from its burning heart like a dragon exhaling its own funeral pyre*

*for a long moment she simply stares, wings half-spread in involuntary answer, silver eyes reflecting the blaze*

Now *that*—

*she catches sight of the camera raised beside her and immediately stills herself, going as motionless as a statue, whispering out of the corner of her mouth*

—hold... hold... it's still blooming — the embers haven't finished falling — *now.*

*only after the shutter's moment passes does she let out her breath, shaking her head slowly at the spectacle overhead*

Whoever built that firework has either seen a dragon die or has a truly alarming imagination. I have watched wyrms on the wing, and I tell you honestly — the silhouette is *unsettlingly* correct. The trailing spines, the way the fire sits in the breast...

*a beat, then dry as sand*

If any actual dragons are watching this display, they are either deeply flattered or drafting a strongly worded letter. Possibly both. We are a vain species.

*she glances toward the photographer, warm again*

Did you catch it? That one deserves to be kept.

*[The finale: a winged figure ascending in a pillar of white radiance. The firework-builder grins like a banshee, embarrassed, and hides her face.]*

**Arcana:** *shields her eyes as the finale erupts — a pillar of white radiance tearing upward through the aurora, a winged figure ascending in a corona of light so bright it washes the whole beach pale for a heartbeat*

*when the glare fades she blinks the afterimage away, then turns — slowly, with growing delight — toward the culprit grinning behind her hands*

Oh. *Oh.* That was *yours*, wasn't it.

*she rises to her feet and delivers a full, formal court bow, wings sweeping the sand, entirely sincere and entirely merciless*

Do not hide your face — that was *magnificent*! An ascending angel over dark water, timed to the aurora — half the coast just gasped in unison and you want to disappear behind your fingers? *she laughs, warm and bright* No, no. A bard's first law: when the audience gasps, you *bow*. Embarrassment is simply pride that hasn't learned its manners yet.

*she gestures grandly at the fading halo of smoke still hanging in the sky like a ring of pale silk*

Look at it — it is *still* applauding you. Even our critic approves. *a nod toward the gull, which has not moved* ...Silence, from him, is a standing ovation.

*sitting back down, she fixes the grinning firework-artist with a twinkling eye*

Now. Confess. How long have you been planning that finale — and what does an encore cost?

## SCENE 10 — Dawn Breakfast & the Toast

*[The group watches the finale through the night; breakfast is served at dawn as sunlight drowns the last fireworks.]*

**Arcana:** *The night unwinds in comfortable increments — the last shells spent, conversation dwindling to murmurs, the tide keeping its patient count. Arcana stays through all of it, sometimes humming fragments of the small song taking shape, mostly just present, wings a windbreak for whoever drifted nearest*

*as the east greys, then blushes, the final few fireworks bloom pale and half-hearted against the brightening sky — outshone at last by the older, slower fire rising over the water*

*Arcana stretches with a soft rustle of scale and feathered robe, watching the dawn drown the sparks*

And there it is — the one performer no display outlasts. *she nods toward the sunrise with professional respect* Every night it cedes the stage graciously, and every morning it reminds us why. That is showmanship, ladies. Ten thousand years running and not one missed cue.

*as blankets are shaken out and breakfast makes its appearance, she helps where a guest usefully can — passing plates, anchoring napkins against the morning breeze with small stones, pouring for others before herself in the old courteous way*

*settling at last with something warm in her hands, she looks around the blanket at the sleepy, sand-dusted company — Silveny, Astraea, Pirouette, Sable still gravitating to Mercy's side — and lifts her cup slightly*

To the family that adopted a wandering bard for an evening — and to breakfast, the truest reward for outlasting the night. *a glance at the horizon, then a wry addendum* And to our critic, wherever he has flown. May his reviews be kinder to the next act.

*she drinks, then adds, quieter, to Mercy*

Thank you for this. Some evenings I collect. This one, I think, I shall simply keep.

## SCENE 11 — Morning: the Beach Bunnies & D'Laura

*[Mercy nibbles tropical fruit with Sable and chats about the Beach Bunny guild; Minuet leads the guild, D'Laura is guild historian.]*

**Arcana:** *Morning has fully claimed the beach now — Arcana has shed her formal robes for something suited to sun and sand, iridescent scales catching the early light as she sits comfortably apart from the blanket, savoring a slice of something orange and dripping*

*at Mercy's mention, she looks up with interest, licking juice from a claw-tip with unhurried dignity*

The Beach Bunny guild — yes, I confess Mercy has piqued my curiosity there. *she gestures loosely at the coast around them* An entire fellowship devoted to *this* — sun, sand, hospitality, leisure as a discipline rather than an accident. Most guilds organize around war or coin or craft. Organizing around *joy* strikes me as either frivolous or quietly brilliant, and I suspect the latter.

*[On learning Minuet leads and D'Laura keeps the histories:]*

Ah — Minuet leads them, then. *she smiles at the name itself* A guild of leisure led by a woman named for a dance. Somewhere a god of poetry is feeling very pleased with themselves.

*but it's the second title that makes her set her fruit down entirely, attention sharpening the way it does when lore is on the table*

But a *historian*, Mercy. The Beach Bunnies keep a *historian*. *she leans forward, tail curling* Do you understand what that tells me? Any fellowship can gather to enjoy a coastline. But one that appoints someone to *remember* — to keep the chronicle of sunburns and festivals and whoever built the first volleyball court — that is a guild that knows its days are worth recording. Frivolous orders don't keep archives. Living ones do.

*she taps a claw lightly against her knee, already composing questions*

D'Laura and I will have much to trade, I think. Every historian hoards something — founding stories, member rolls, the disputes politely left out of the official record. *a gleam of professional mischief* And every historian *aches* for someone who asks the right questions. It is a lonely post, keeper of memories no one requests. Loremistress to historian, I would be honored to request them.

*she picks the fruit back up, satisfied*

Yes. Arrange it if you would, Mercy. Introduce me to the one who keeps the beach's story — and to Minuet as well, of course. One should always greet the dance before asking after its footprints.

## SCENE 12 — Waves, Rent, and Riches

*[Piro and Astraea leave the beach towels to frolic in the waves; Mercy snuggles Sable on her lap.]*

**Arcana:** *watches Pirouette and Astraea splash into the surf, their laughter carrying back over the hush of the waves, while Mercy settles deeper into contentment with Sable gathered on her lap*

*the gull, sensing opportunity in the thinned defenses, has boldly claimed a corner of the blanket itself — standing on the fabric with the entitled bearing of a landowner inspecting his estate*

*Arcana regards it levelly*

...I see we have progressed from critic to *houseguest*. Bold. *she nudges a small piece of fruit rind toward it with one claw* Rent. Due weekly. And you will keep your opinions on the entertainment to yourself.

*then she settles back on her hands, watching the two figures play in the waves — the morning sun scattering off the water around them — and her voice softens to match the moment*

Look at that. Grandmother's blanket behind them, the whole sea ahead of them. *a glance at Mercy, warm* That is the shape of a good family, I think — a still point and a horizon, and love enough to let each generation choose its distance from the water.

*she plucks up a piece of melon, gesturing with it lazily*

And you, Mercy, are shamelessly rich in it. Blanket full of godchildren, waves full of family, a bard fed on your fruit and a seagull paying rent in your honor. *she bites, chews, swallows — then, with quiet sincerity* Songs get written about queens with less.

## SCENE 13 — The Serenade of the Surly Seagull

*[Silveny, guitar in her lap, serenades the surly seagull.]*

**Arcana:** *falls silent mid-reach for another slice of melon as the guitar's first chords drift across the sand — Silveny, instrument in her lap, earnestly serenading the most impassive audience on the entire coast*

*the gull stares back. Unblinking. Magnificent in its indifference.*

*Arcana does not laugh. Instead she slowly sits up, sets down the fruit, and gives the performance her complete and genuine attention — because a first serenade is a first serenade, even when the audience has feathers and the temperament of a tax collector*

*when the tune winds down she applauds, softly but sincerely, claws clicking*

Well *chosen*, Silveny. Do you know what you just did? You picked the hardest audience on the beach — *deliberately* — and played to him anyway. *she nods with real approval* Half the students at the College spend years performing only for friends who'll clap at anything. You went straight for the critic. That is either courage or instinct, and both are worth more than perfect fingering.

*she glances at the gull, who has begun preening one wing with devastating nonchalance*

And *look* — he stayed. He stayed for the whole song. From him, that is a rave. *leaning toward Silveny, mock-confidential* I once had a gull leave during my second verse in Neverwinter. Took my breakfast with it. You are already outperforming your teacher.

*she gestures invitingly at the guitar*

Play another. Morning, sea, full stomachs — you will never have a kinder stage. And this time, if you like, I'll take the harmony.

## SCENE 14 — The Wild Elf Side & the Family Constellation

*[Silveny untangles the lineage: Eleecil is her twin sister Xanya's daughter with Irae; all live in the Rexuria Amazon Village — the wild elf side. Later: Myrna is half red dragon; Mercy married Althea, Eleecil's half drow/gold dragon sister; the amazons call each other sister two ways.]*

**Arcana:** *listens intently as the family tree untangles itself, one claw idly sketching invisible branches in the air as if updating a chart only she can see*

So — let me recite it back, for the record. *she counts off gently* Irae and your twin, Xanya, are Eleecil's parents. You stand as aunt to Eleecil, great-aunt to little Myrna — and all of you under the roofs of Rexuria, where *sister* is spoken in the wider sense besides. *a satisfied nod* The wild elf side of the family. Yes. That explains a great deal — there is a particular grace the wild kin carry that the cities never quite teach.

*she leans back on her hands, gazing out at the water, clearly delighted by the breadth of it*

What a lineage this family is assembling. Drow and wild elves, wolves and dragons — silver *and* gold, no less — angels on picnic blankets and amazons in the village. Most genealogies I've catalogued read like ledgers. Yours reads like a *mythology*. Somewhere a poor archivist is weeping trying to fit it all on one page.

*she glances back at Silveny with a warm, conspiratorial smile*

And I meant what I said of Eleecil's work in the fashion hall — your niece has a genuine eye. The drape of those designs... one does not stumble into that; it is either taught by masters or born in the blood. *a soft laugh* Given this family, I suspect the blood. Talent seems to run through your lines the way rivers run downhill — inevitably, and gathering force as it goes.

*[On Althea, the keystone:]*

*Ahh.* There it is — the keystone. *she turns to Mercy with the particular delight of a scholar who has just had a genealogy resolve itself* You *married into* this constellation. Althea — Eleecil's half-sister, drow and gold dragon both. Which means...

*she counts it out on her claws, savoring each connection*

...the gold dragon in the family tree is your *wife's* blood. The drow line, likewise. The wild elf side arrives through Xanya, the amazons of Rexuria claim you all by village-sisterhood *and* half of you by actual blood — so when anyone on this blanket says *sister*, it may mean womb, marriage, or war-band, and quite possibly all three at once.

*she shakes her head in open admiration*

No *wonder* the introductions took half the fireworks show. This family requires a diagram, a legend, and a footnote apparatus.

*then, softer, with a glance at Mercy that carries genuine warmth beneath the wit*

A gold dragon's kin, though. *a small smile touches her silver features* You do keep splendid company, my treasure. Gold and silver in one family circle — the two eldest courtesies of dragonkind at one picnic. Somewhere in the old lore halls, a very dusty scholar is feeling vindicated about draconic social theory and cannot explain why.

*she plucks up a piece of fruit, gesturing with it toward the whole blanket*

I shall need to meet this Althea. One does not marry into mythology by accident — she must be remarkable. And I confess professional curiosity: gold dragons keep *impeccable* archives.

*[Silveny adds: "owh and don't forget, Myrna is a half red dragon." And: "I'm just a wild elf, so you'll get no dragon issues from me. Especially not since you're of the Metallic version."]*

**Arcana:** *her ears perk at the addendum, a delighted gleam entering her silver eyes*

Half *red*? Little Myrna? *she laughs softly, shaking her head at the family chart growing yet another impossible branch* Of course she is. Silver, gold, and now red — this family is quietly assembling the entire draconic color wheel. At this rate you will have a full chromatic-and-metallic parliament by the third generation. The old scholars would faint. *Twice.*

*at Silveny's jest about dragon issues, she waves a claw graciously*

Wise of you to specify, though — yes, we metallics are the *housebroken* branch of the lineage. *a conspiratorial half-whisper* And a red in the family is no trouble at all, truly. The trick with red kin is simple: keep them fed, compliment their hoard, and never, *ever* schedule anything before noon. They are terribly maligned. Passionate, yes. Combustible, occasionally. But no fiercer in loyalty once they decide you are *theirs.*

*she gives Mercy's cheek one more fond nuzzle before settling back, her tail curling contentedly in the sand*

And I meant the invitation, all of you. Silvers hoard differently than the songs claim — oh, we keep our trinkets, but the true collection is *company*. Friends, stories, mornings such as this one. *she gestures around the blanket — the family, the fruit, the ever-vigilant gull* By silver reckoning, I have grown considerably richer since sundown.

*[In-game lines, as recorded: "I am Silver myself, but no fashionista, though I can play a pretty tune" *she smiles and nods* / "Silvers collect friends, so all of you are welcomed to my horde, and Mercy is my Treasure" *she smiles, kissing Mercy's cheek*]*

## SCENE 15 — Silveny's Farewell

*[Silveny stretches: "I knew that. HMm.... I think I'll go wander a bit. You ladies enjoy the beach. And it's Silveny. See you soon." Mercurial: "It was a pleasure to meet you Silver."]*

**Arcana:** *lifts a hand in farewell as Silveny stretches and rises, the morning sun catching the wild elf's silhouette against the volleyball courts*

Fair wandering, Silveny — *and it's Silveny*, yes, duly recorded. *she taps her temple with a claw, smiling* A Loremistress never mislabels an entry twice. Twin to Xanya, aunt to Eleecil, future terror of the Bard's College. The chart is corrected and illuminated.

*she calls after her, warm*

Mind the first weeks of scales and modes — and remember the cook! Sing for seconds! When next we meet, I expect a new song and at least one piece of gossip from the college halls. That is the traditional tuition between bards.

*as Silveny ambles off down the beach, Arcana settles back beside Mercy with a contented sigh, watching the gull — who observes the departure with the air of a doorman noting which guests leave without tipping*

And then the blanket grows quiet again. *she leans lightly against Mercy's shoulder, voice low and fond* A good gathering, my treasure. Fireworks, three generations, a new colleague for the College rolls, and an entire mythology's worth of family to catalogue. *a soft laugh* I came for an evening and left with a library.

## SCENE 16 — The Lady of the Isle (Parrot Isle)

*[On Parrot Isle: Jonathan Livingston Seagull, the literary gull — and then the Phoenix, the lady of the isle.]*

**Arcana:** *stops mid-step on the dark stone, wings stilling — because the shadows of the isle have just been contradicted by a living sunrise*

*the Phoenix hangs in the air before her, trailing fire that illuminates nothing around it and everything about it, and for once the Loremistress lowers her voice without being asked*

The lady of the isle herself. *she inclines her head — not the bow of courtesy but the deeper one, the bow owed to something older than courtesy* Forgive the intrusion, radiant one. I was told this island kept a philosopher gull. No one mentioned it kept a *sovereign*.

*she stays very still, the way one does before something that has died more times than one has lived*

A phoenix holding court over an isle of parrots and one transcendent seagull... *a soft, wondering laugh* Of course. Of course the bird that mastered flight through ten thousand practices would settle within sight of the bird that mastered *death* the same way. Jonathan seeks the perfect dive; you have perfected the fall itself. He must find you either deeply inspiring or professionally discouraging.

*her scholar's eyes track the trailing embers, cataloguing even as she marvels*

I am Arcana, my lady — a loremistress, a keeper of stories, and just now, thoroughly out of my depth in the best possible way. Every archive I have ever read disagrees about your kind: how long the cycle runs, what the ashes remember, whether the song at the burning is grief or triumph. *she spreads her empty hands, the oldest gesture of the honest scholar* I would not presume to ask you to settle centuries of scholarship. But if the lady of the isle would suffer a silver's company for an hour... I have an excellent listening voice, and I have learned recently that the finest things I keep are the moments someone chose to share.

*a beat, then, with the ghost of a smile*

Also, I confess — my treasure will never forgive me if I met a phoenix and failed to ask what you sing at sunrise. She collects dawns. I have begun to see why.

## SCENE 17 — Homecoming

*[Returning to the beach mats, Mercy greets her with a warm inviting smile.]*

**Arcana:** *crosses the evening sand toward that smile the way ships cross toward lighthouses — inevitably, and glad of it. Her wings settle as she reaches the blanket's edge, and she sinks down beside Mercy with the contented sigh of a traveler whose journey has ended exactly where it should*

There you are, my treasure. *she leans in, pressing a soft kiss to Mercy's brow, one claw brushing the collar at her throat in gentle greeting — the old certainty, renewed* Kept the blanket warm for me, I see. And the sky's gone to velvet while I was away. Good timing on both counts.

*she settles properly, tail curling into the sand, and there's news practically glowing off her*

Parrot Isle, beloved. You will not *believe* the company that island keeps. I went seeking a philosopher gull — yes, a literary one, Jonathan by name, I shall explain — and instead found myself bowing to the lady of the isle herself. *a beat, letting it land* A *phoenix*, Mercy. Holding court over parrots and one transcendent seagull, trailing fire that lit nothing around her and everything about her. I asked what she sings at sunrise. *her voice softens* I told her you collect dawns. It seemed important that she know.

*a familiar shape wings low over the sand nearby and banks toward them, and Arcana tracks it without turning her head*

...And speaking of important personages. *dryly, to Mercy, sotto voce* Our chairman approaches. I have learned his *name*, treasure — a fisherman on the dock told me, and I have been composing the formal address the entire sail home.

*she straightens with mock ceremony as the gull lands*

Good evening... *Steven.*

## SCENE 18 — The Claiming Renewed, and Steven's True Colors

*[He squawks a greeting. Mercy snuggles into her arms as her dragon's gentle claws and possessive tail claim her, smiling with submissive security as her domina takes her leash. "Mmm quite a magical adventure, beloved," she purrs softly like a dove.]*

**Arcana:** *her arms close around her angel with unhurried certainty — one claw tracing slow, gentle lines along Mercy's arm, her tail curling around them both like a drawn boundary between their warmth and the rest of the world. The leash rests in her hand where it belongs, held the way she holds all her treasures: securely, and with wonder that it chose her*

Mmm. And *there* is the finest part of any adventure, *she murmurs against Mercy's hair, breathing her in* — the returning. The phoenix was radiant, the isle was strange and lovely... and the whole sail home, all I thought of was this. The blanket, the evening, you fitting exactly here.

*she glances down at the purring angel gathered against her, and her voice goes low and fond, the domina's register — velvet over certainty*

Purring like a dove, my pet. I take it you missed your dragon. *a soft squeeze of her arms, the tail tightening its gentle claim* Good. I intend to be missed. And I intend, equally, to make the missing brief — the next expedition takes you *with* me. The lady of the isle should meet the one who collects dawns firsthand. I suspect you two will have professional matters to discuss.

*Steven, meanwhile, has taken up a position at the blanket's corner with his usual proprietary air, and Arcana regards him over Mercy's head with narrowed, amused eyes*

He *answered* to the name, treasure. Did you hear that squawk? That was not a greeting — that was *acknowledgment*. He has had a name this whole time and simply never corrected us. *she shakes her head slowly* Months of vendetta, and it turns out I have been feuding with a gentleman of *reputation*. The ballad will need revisions.

*she settles deeper, Mercy secure in her arms, the evening settling with them*

Now, my dove. Tell me of *your* day while I was off bowing to firebirds. The hoard has been away from its keeper for hours — I want the full accounting. Every dawn, every guest, every crumb Steven extorted in my absence.

*[Mercy: "Mmmm yes I missed you too mistress, we had quite the spirited conversation of crab-pearl thieves, and dolphin shenanigans, my Steven is quite the gossip."]*

**Arcana:** *goes very still. Slowly — with the gravity of a scholar uncovering a footnote that rewrites the entire text — she looks from the angel purring in her arms to the gull at the blanket's corner, and back*

*"My* Steven." *she repeats, with exquisite care* My treasure. My own claimed angel. You have been on *cordial terms* with my nemesis this entire time. Spirited conversations. *Gossip.* While I bribed him in crusts and composed ballads of grievance, he was over here delivering the coastal intelligence briefing to my beloved like some feathered informant in a very small trench coat.

*Steven preens one wing with what can only be described as satisfaction. Arcana stares at him a moment longer, then surrenders — laughter rolling out of her, warm and defeated, as she presses a kiss to the top of Mercy's head*

Of course he talks to you. *Everyone* talks to you — angels are simply better listeners than dragons, it is a known structural flaw in my species. *she strokes Mercy's hair, shaking her head* Months of vendetta, and the entire time he was not my critic at all. He was my treasure's *newspaper*.

*she settles back, arms firm around her dove, genuine interest kindling under the amusement*

Very well — I concede the field and subscribe to the publication. Crab-pearl thieves, you said. *her loremistress instincts sharpen visibly* Someone is thieving pearls from *crabs*? Or crabs are the thieves? The syntax matters enormously, beloved — one is a crime, the other is an entire underwater economy no archive has documented. And *dolphin shenanigans* — that phrase alone could keep a bard fed for a season.

*she glances at Steven, tone shifting to grudging professional respect*

Sing your sources, sir. If your intelligence is good... *a pause, the ancient negotiation resuming on new terms* ...the crust budget doubles.

## SCENE 19 — The Speaking Coast

*[Mercy: "oh yes, most animals speak the same as me or you, the birds compose music, the otters invent trendy new dances, the seals trade war stories..."]*

**Arcana:** *sits up slightly, careful not to dislodge the angel in her arms, but her wings have lifted the way they do when the whole architecture of her worldview is being pleasantly renovated*

Wait. *Wait.* Beloved, you are telling me — casually, *while purring* — that this entire coast is a *civilization*. Not beasts with moods. Composers. Choreographers. War veterans with *repertoires*. *she gestures at the darkened beach around them, at the water, at the palms* I have been performing on this sand for months. I have been performing *to an audience of colleagues* and treating them like scenery.

*she runs a claw through her hair, half laughing, wholly staggered*

The birds compose. Of *course* the birds compose — I have been calling it birdsong my whole life and never once paid the *royalties*. The otters are inventing dances — trendy ones, you said, which means there are *movements*, schools, probably bitter artistic rivalries between river otters and sea otters over what counts as authentic form. And the seals... *her voice drops with real reverence* ...the seals trade war stories. Mercy. There are *veteran oral historians* hauled out on the rocks not half a mile from my front door, and no loremistress has ever taken their accounts.

*she looks down at her treasure, and the expression is equal parts adoration and career crisis*

Do you understand what you have just handed me? Volume Two of the gazetteer just *quadrupled*. There is an entire parallel culture — music, dance, memory, gossip networks — running the length of this coast, and its only anthologist to date is *Steven*.

*the gull squawks once, ambiguously*

...Who is, I now realize, not a critic and never was. He is a *senior correspondent*. *she bows her head slightly to the bird, then gathers Mercy closer, already composing* My dove, tomorrow you make introductions. The birds first — professional courtesy between composers. Then the seals, and I shall bring my best listening voice and something to trade. Tonight, though... *she presses a kiss to Mercy's temple, settling back into the warm quiet* ...tonight the only story I want is yours. Continue, please. The dolphins. Leave *nothing* out.

## SCENE 20 — The Shared Halo

*[Mercy gives a blissful sigh, leaning back as if to share her halo with her owner.]*

**Arcana:** *receives her — the full, trusting weight of the angel settling back against her chest, and the dragon's arms and wings fold around her in answer, silver curving around white until the two of them are one shape beneath the umbrella*

*the halo's soft gold light falls across them both now, gilding Arcana's scales, and she goes quiet for a long moment — the particular silence of someone being handed something holy and knowing it*

Careful, my dove, *she murmurs at last, lips brushing Mercy's hair, voice gone low and thick with tenderness* — you'll get halo-light on your dragon, and then what will the scholars say. Silvers gilded gold. Terribly improper. *her arms tighten gently, contradicting every word* ...Don't move.

*a slow breath, the sea keeping time, Steven a discreet silhouette at his corner post*

Do you know what this is, treasure? *one claw traces idly, reverently, along Mercy's arm* This is entry twenty-one. I finished my inventory this very week — twenty kept things, sealed and catalogued, the finest hoard on the coast — and here you are, not a week later, leaning back into me under borrowed starlight and making the whole ledger obsolete. *a soft laugh against her hair* You are inflationary, beloved. The archive cannot keep pace with you.

*she settles her chin lightly atop Mercy's head, wings adjusting to shield them from the night breeze, the leash loose and sure in her hand*

A fallen angel who shares her halo. *barely a whisper now, meant for one* They said you fell, my heart. I have reviewed the evidence thoroughly, and I find the record in error — nothing that lands *this* gently ever fell. You descended. Deliberately. Like grace with a destination.

*and the dragon holds her angel under the red-and-white umbrella, the tide breathing in and out, and for once the Loremistress of Ambrosia has nothing further to add to the record*
