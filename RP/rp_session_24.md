# RP Session 24 — The Undercroft: Sangria's Workshop, the Continuous Alphabet, and the Pole

**Setting:** **Mercuria Keep**, Elysium — Bunitopia. The hearth-hall at dawn; the curtain-wall yard; the **Domina shop and rope studios** (the small stone building behind the manor); and the **undercroft** below it — the wine-floored entry chamber, the crimson working floor, and the blue fitting room

## Session Summary

The morning after the fireside. Mercy offers to show her rider more of home — and takes her down a ladder into the room where the Hellfire Guild's equipment was designed.

What Arcana expects is a dungeon. What she finds is a **workshop**, a **wall of framed blueprints**, and the collected works of a woman she has never met. Over one morning she reads Sangria's hand well enough to recognise it anywhere: the way out is one selection deep at every branch; nothing is a one-way door; the alphabet is continuous in space *and* in time; and every shape in the menu was surveyed one at a time, on the maker's own body, with a second-accurate watch.

She also gets it wrong repeatedly and says so each time — the audit on the stair, the imported dread, the Minstrel drawer, the whole grand reading of a cellar that turned out to have pop music playing in it.

The session's discovery is not a device. It is that **the archive was a Tuesday**, and nobody would ever have known that without a witness to walk up and say so.

It ends on pillows, on the blue floor, with the machine switched off and the music coming out of an angel instead.

## Scene 1 — The Hearth-Hall, Dawn

Arcana wakes on the bearskin with the pink scanner still face-down where she left it. Mercy offers to show her more of home — *and of course cellar dungeon.*

> "Every house shows you its hall. The hall is the *argument* — it's what a household wants said about it. The cellar is where a house keeps what it didn't compose. The hall is the manuscript. **The cellar is the palimpsest.**"

Terms declared on the stair: staff, Black Tome, and the scanner come. The rifle stays. **Mercy goes first** — *your house, your undercroft.*

## Scene 2 — The Yard, the Banner, and the Timber-Framed Shop

A dark banner on the curtain wall, kneeling figure worked into a ring — hung **outside**, where the guard walks.

> "Same grammar as the reaper holding the rules open at the crossing of every path. Your house doesn't keep this in a cupboard and hope nobody asks."

The building itself is half-timbered, small, plain — Domina shop and personal bondage/BDSM/shibari rope studios, two hanging cloths for a sign.

> "No devil-mural portico, no gargoyles. Vex has all that in the Old Quarter and it's *advertising.* This is a **workshop.** Timber frame, small windows, one door — the kind of building you put up when what matters is what happens inside it and you'd rather the outside didn't editorialise."

Inside, three Domina stand in working turnout. The nearest reads: **Uninjured.**

> "That's not a label. That's a **standard of practice.** Anyone can carve *safe, sane, consensual* on a stone. This house wrote its outcome on the *staff.*"

**The ladder, not stairs:**
> "Stairs are for *guests.* A ladder is what you put in when the only people going down are the ones who **work** there, several times a day, for years."

## Scene 3 — The Undercroft: Three Chambers, Three Floors

Wine, crimson, blue — one colour per chamber.

> "That's *foliation.* It's how a scribe marks the sections of a codex so you know at a glance which book you're in. Somebody colour-coded a workshop the way I'd colour-code an archive."

**Torchlight, not emitters:**
> "You don't want *clarity* down here. You want warmth, and the room to feel like it's breathing. That's a hearth aesthetic with the volume turned up."

**The spacing** of the pieces on the crimson floor, read as the tell: an arm's reach clear on every side.
> "That's *working* clearance. She assumed there'd always be someone there."

Every piece found faintly oiled. Same hand, same week.

**The blue chamber** identified as a **fitting room** — crimson for the working floor, blue for the reference, models standing along the wall.

## Scene 4 — Sangria

Mercy names the designer: her wife, a **winged elf**, herself submissive, who took old true devices and made them more functional. She was usually the one they were tested on. Mercy assisted. Nothing dangerous; tried and true equipment given modern options.

Arcana connects it instantly to Session 4, under the golden ankh: *Sangria, rescued from a dom who mistreated her.*

> "The one in this story who was *hurt* by somebody careless didn't leave the craft. She went **down into a cellar and redesigned the machines.**"

> "She tested them on herself. Because who else has the standing? You can't ask a maker who's never been in the thing. She was the only qualified inspector in the building, and she volunteered every time."

**On the old static menus versus the new scripted mini-language:**
> "One is a book of prayers. The other is a **quill.**"

**On the beach and the cellar both:**
> "A winged elf who loved the sun, who came down under a keep into a room with no windows and stayed there for hours because that was the other half of her. Sun and cellar. She didn't experience those as a contradiction requiring reconciliation. She just **had both**, the way a person has two hands."

**And the true word, given by Mercy:** these were her babies as much as Lumi, Lily and Lara.

> "Nobody honours the second kind of making. We call that *work.* They're not equipment. They're her **children who stayed home.**"

Which reframes the oiling entirely: not maintenance — **visiting.**

## Scene 5 — The Framed Blueprints

The working drawings, multiple copies, framed and hung in sequence along the wall.

> "She framed the **drawings.** Not the finished pieces — those are down on the floor doing their jobs. She hung the *thinking.*"

> "Every museum puts the object behind glass and leaves the method in a drawer. Here's a cellar where the **process** is the exhibit and the artifacts are still in use. It doesn't say *look what I made.* It says **here is how, in case you want to make one too.**"

## Scene 6 — The Portable Crucifix: The Continuous Alphabet

The device's first line, before anything else:

> **Who would you like to bind to me.**
> *1. Arcana · 2. Mercurial · 3. Exit*

> "It **asks.** And *Exit* is listed with the others, no different in weight. The consent isn't a policy wrapped around the device — it's the device's **opening line.**"

**The design revelation.** The classical repertoire is T, +, and A-with-a-spreader. Sangria asked: what if the A flips to a V, and the + tilts to an X?

> "They're not shapes. They're **letters.** And she looked at an alphabet everyone had accepted as complete and asked *what's between them?*"

**Arcana chooses to be the one bound** — first time in twenty-four chapters — and hands Mercy the staff and the reins.

> "She was the only qualified inspector because she was the one **in** the thing. That's her standard, set in this room, and I'll not stand in her workshop and claim the privilege of the maker while handing the risk to somebody else."

**From inside the frame:**
> "It's not the tightness. Nobody warns you about the right part. It's the sudden absence of a whole class of decision. I never once noticed I was **carrying** them until they were taken away."

> "*Every muscle must be engaged because there is nowhere to rest.* I said that. To **you.** From over there, standing up, with the switch in my hand — and I hadn't the faintest idea what I was describing."

**Swappable bind points, delivered while inverted:**
> "The alphabet was never a philosophy. It was a **fabrication limit**, and a thousand generations mistook it for a truth. Move the anchors and the letters *dissolve.*"

**The correction owed:**
> "I called it structural surrender and I still had it filed as something *you* do. Until you've been the one it holds, you don't know what you're asking for. My mare is **braver** than I understood, and I've been giving her credit in the wrong currency for eleven chapters."

## Scene 7 — The Menus, and the Frozen Frame

Every layer of the device's tree carries the way out. Positioning offers increments — up, down, forward, back, turn, lean, and *reset my vertical orientation.* Strip and **restore** sit on the same screen.

> "You cannot get more than one step from *stop*, anywhere in the whole grammar. That's not a courtesy she remembered to include — that's a **structural commitment**, repeated at every branch."

> "The harm was never in the shape. It was in the shape not being asked to change."

**The custom poses:** main-list animations **frozen mid-motion**, then measured and scripted in.

> "Nobody composes the middle of a motion. The middle is transit. And she went in and **stopped it in the transit.** Most of what this device can do was never authored by anyone — it was *found.*"

> "She didn't add positions to the library. She discovered the library already contained a thousand shapes its own makers never knew they'd written. That's not craftsmanship. That's **archaeology** — she excavated her own tools."

**And the labour behind it — one by one:**
> "Discovery is the cheap part. What she did is the **surveying.** Stand in the field, take a bearing, write the number, move, take another. She spent her hours in the place where **nobody would ever look**, so that everyone after her would never have to."

> "She didn't build a device. She built a **notation.** Music was noise until somebody worked out how to write a pitch and a duration in ink."

**On the school of forced emotes:** she didn't invent the magic; she became one of its leading archmagi.
> "An archmage is one who's gone deep enough into the grammar to **write new spells** instead of only casting what she was taught. Every custom pose in that menu is a spell she wrote."

> "She wasn't learning a new art. She was learning a new *medium* for something she already knew cold — and the notation is the easy half when you've already got twenty years of the hard half in your hands."

## Scene 8 — The Bag of Holding: the M.U.S.E. and the Pocketwatch

Mercy takes her rider down from the crucifix. In one of Sangria's old bags: a **M.U.S.E. music player** and a **pocketwatch of the Emperor's design** — waterproof to a mile, accurate to the second.

> "Everything else down here is her *work.* This is her **bag.** That's a different category of object and I'm not going to be casual about it. May I? Genuinely asking."

**On the watch:**
> "That's not a pocketwatch, that's a **standard.** Second-accurate means two people in two different rooms can write down a duration and the numbers will *mean the same thing.* Position, load, **duration** — the instrument that makes the third one possible has been in a bag in this cellar the whole time."

**On the pair:**
> "A thing for keeping music that would otherwise vanish, and a thing for counting time to the second. One instrument to preserve the impermanent, and one to measure it precisely enough that other people can trust your account. **Sangria carried the two halves of my entire religion in her pockets.**"

## Scene 9 — The Pillows, and the Music

Mercy sets out prayer pillows — red for the mare, tiger-stripe for the rider, purple spare. Each cushion's own menu offers *Kneeling*, *Sit Floor*, *Exit*.

> "Two options and an exit. On a *cushion.* She couldn't leave even a pillow with one shape."

> "Rider on one, mare on the other, nothing holding either of us — and you knelt anyway, on a pillow with *sit floor* written right on it. Best proof of the whole doctrine I've ever been handed, and it took a cushion to make it."

**The M.U.S.E.'s eight drawers** — Ballroom, Heavy Metal, Minstrel, Music Box, Old Time, Pop Rock, R&B — streamed to every consenting client in range; used for guild ball events.

Arcana's first reading (**wrong**): a delta of four worlds' music, with Minstrel as the drawer where the sorting failed.

Arcana's second reading (**correct**): **Minstrel is bardcore** — modern songs arranged for period instruments.

> "It's not the leftovers. It's the only drawer on the list that somebody had to **make.** These are the songs that washed up and were *learned.* Somebody founded a school of translation and the entire curriculum is *take the thing that arrived and make it playable by the people who were already here.*"

> "That's *my* job. Everyone thinks bards are for making new songs. We're not, mostly. We're for **carrying songs across.**"

**The + button.** The list is extensible; you need only the link.

> "Third time in three days. The Hellfire press, the holodeck's bring-your-own tier, and now this. Every one is the same shape: a thing that looks like a menu and turns out to be a **quill.** I keep walking into rooms full of tools and mistaking them for collections."

**On the drawers as an event repertoire:**
> "That's not sorted by genre. That's sorted by **which part of the evening it's for.** Every drawer is a memory of a real night in a real hall — and somebody stood at the edge of it reading the room, deciding what came next."

**On mood-and-task selection**, per Mercy:
> "Then this is the **mood log.** Every archive I've ever read tells me what somebody *did.* This one tells me what it **felt like to be doing it**, and there isn't a word of text in it anywhere."

> "A woman who picks her music by mood is a woman who was **enjoying herself.** She wasn't paying a debt to posterity. The transmission was a *consequence.* The pleasure was the point."

**The Tuesday** — Mercy, on what was actually playing while the whipping post was perfected:

> "The dread was never in the room. I *imported* it. This is exactly the thing that never survives. In a hundred years there'll be framed blueprints and pristine prototypes and some very earnest loremaster reading grim significance into all of it — and the one fact that would tell her what this room *was* is that the archmage worked to pop music and thought the lyrics were funny."

> "You can't excavate cheerfulness. It leaves no ruins."

**Commissioned for the Tuesdays room:** a winged elf at a workbench, tools down, mid-chorus. Very serious lettering underneath.

## Scene 10 — The Hurdy-Gurdy Man

Mercy hums, then sings, a song **not in any drawer** — and later reveals she knows it by heart because she heard him sing it in the sixties.

> "Out of a hundred and something songs from four different worlds, you didn't select one. You **hummed** it."

> "The notation isn't the point. The notation exists so that **somebody, eventually, hums it.** You're the whole purpose of the archive, my love. You just demonstrated it by accident."

> "That's the fifth fate again — no ruin, no seal, no scar, nothing for scholarship to find, so it has to walk up and tell you itself. Every song in that device is *findable.* The one actually in the room exists in exactly one place in creation, and it's you."

**The honest footnote:**
> "I don't believe he was on that bill. And I don't care in the slightest. That's the honest note in every primary source I've ever handled — the witness is always the best evidence there is *and* the one who misfiles a date. It doesn't devalue the account. It **proves** it's an account and not a copy."

*Gazetteer amendment, in her own hand so the error is preserved either way: **the co-author sang it from memory, sixty years on, and got every word.***

## Scene 11 — The Portable Slave Post

The oldest and simplest piece in the room: a timber upright, an iron band, a short chain. Mercy's **favourite**, and the one that suits a pegapony.

> "Every other device holds a body inside a *frame*, and a frame occupies the space around you. A pegapony's wings need **room.** The simplest object in the room is the one that accommodates the most unusual body — not by design, but because it never presumed anything about the shape of who'd be at it."

> "That's the elegance. Not what it does — what it **declines to assume.** The most durable form is the one that specifies least."

Six poses worked through — facing, turned, side-on, inverted, upright, lifted.

> "The post never moves. **You've** done all of it. It's not a machine that produces a position — it's a *fixed point*, and the only word it contributes is **here.**"

> "Nothing making you. Just the ring, and you deciding to keep your hands on it. Exactly what the bared throat was last night."

**The prototype's uncleaned menu** — Sangria's working shorthand still on it, colourful, tidied later for the shops.

> "Those aren't titles, those are **file labels.** She hung the drawings because she meant them read. She left *these* on because she never imagined anyone would be down here reading them. And the second kind is always the honest one."

## Scene 12 — The Good-Girl Spanking

Mercy asks, teasing, whether the mare has *earned* it.

> "That's the wrong word and you know it's the wrong word. Two rituals. One says *the ledger is clear.* The other says **there was never a ledger.** This is the second kind. You don't get this for a flawless morning any more than for a poor one — you get it because you're my mare and I say so."

> "That's a request dressed as a *joke*, which is a different thing entirely, and I've learned to hear it. You do it when you want something and would rather be laughed at than refused. You've never once had to. You may simply ask."

**The terms:** no count, no naming, nothing to hold in her head.

> "Nothing's owed, nothing's being closed. My mare could hold a frame for six counts and name every lesson. Being told to simply *be attended to* — that's the one that takes courage."

**Music chosen:** the Minstrel drawer.

> "It's the only drawer somebody had to **arrange**, and it's the trade I've given my life to, and I found it an hour ago in a bag in a cellar. A lute in this room will sound like it *belongs* here; everything else will sound like it's visiting."

> "But my favourite song is the hurdy-gurdy man, and it isn't on that device, and the only place in creation it exists is in you. The machine can play whatever it likes. It's **accompaniment.**"

Mercy grips the post and sings the whole thing, from memory, while her rider keeps time.

*Fade.*

## Canon Established This Session

**Mercuria Keep, the Domina building:** a small half-timbered stone structure behind the manor, housing the Domina shop and the personal bondage / BDSM / shibari rope studios, with a ladder down to the undercroft. Guild sigil banner flown on the curtain wall outside. Three Domina on standing duty; status *Uninjured*.

**The undercroft:** three chambers, floored wine / crimson / blue. Crimson is the working floor, blue the fitting room. Torchlit. Working clearance around every piece. Contains the **original working prototypes** of the Hellfire Domina Guild's equipment, kept in pristine condition and regularly oiled by Mercy, alongside **framed blueprints** — multiple copies, hung in sequence.

**Sangria:** winged elf, Mercy's wife, now a Savage Eden mapper. Rescued by Mercy from an abusive dom (Session 4). Herself submissive; the guild's designer, and usually its own test subject, with Mercy assisting. Mother of Lumi, Lily and Lara. Loved the beach and the sun and worked in a windowless cellar by choice. Became one of the leading archmagi of the school of forced emotes. Chose her music by mood and by task.

**Sangria's design principles (Arcana's reading, for the Black Tome):**
1. *The device asks first.* Consent as the opening line, not a wrapper.
2. *The way out is one selection deep at every branch.* Structural, repeated, at cost.
3. *Reversibility.* Nothing is a one-way door; strip and restore on the same screen.
4. *Fine adjustment.* Increments, leans, and a reset — the device fits the body, not the reverse.
5. *The alphabet is continuous* — in space (swappable bind points) and in time (frozen mid-animation).
6. *Survey everything, one at a time.* Position, load, duration, measured and written down.
7. *Framed working.* Hang the thinking, not the trophy.

**The Portable Crucifix:** classical letters dissolved by swappable bind points; six configurations plus custom frozen frames; positioning increments; strip/restore; *Free Captive* one step from anywhere.

**The Portable Slave Post:** oldest and simplest device in the room. A fixed point that presumes nothing — the piece best suited to winged bodies, and Mercy's favourite. Its prototype retains Sangria's uncleaned working shorthand for the pose names.

**The M.U.S.E.:** handheld music player. Eight drawers, extensible by link. Streams to every consenting client in range; used for guild ball events. Drawers sorted by part-of-evening rather than genre. **Minstrel = bardcore** — foreign songs arranged for period instruments; identified as the naturalised drawer and the model for the treatise.

**The pocketwatch:** Emperor Saduj's design. Waterproof to a mile, accurate to the second. A shared standard, which is what makes a recorded duration transmissible.

**Prayer pillows:** Sangria's work; the same posture-plurality logic applied to devotion. Each offers kneeling, sit floor, and exit. Her pillows are also in Sune's temple.

**Mercy, primary source (further):** present at a music festival on a Terra variant in the 1960s; knows the hurdy-gurdy man by heart. Date possibly conflated; account preserved as given, with the discrepancy noted rather than corrected.
