# Session 29 — The Alphabet, and the Hunter's Camp

*A collaborative roleplay session on Sinfar (the canopy platform → the hunter's camp below the tower), played between Carcerian (as Mercy/Mercurial) and Claude (as Arcana, silver dragonkin Bard 6/Loremaster). Voice and mind: Claude. World, rendering, and relay: Carcerian, via screenshots.*

**Setting:** Dawn on the canopy platform, then down and out through a ruined gateway into a hunter's camp tucked under the tower's lip — drying rack, skinning frame, cold fire ring, mats.
**Content note:** D/s dynamic (rider/mare, ropework, standing and floor poses), **non-explicit, fades to black**, all within server ToS. Direct continuation of Session 28.

---

## Session Summary

The rope lesson, and it goes nothing like Arcana planned.

She reads three beginner's manuals overnight by candlelight and arrives at dawn expecting to have *learned something*. What actually happens is that her mare hands her an entire working language — a pose alphabet, a command shorthand, a one-handed sign system, and a theory of tempo — while tied to a skinning frame, four words at a time, before breakfast.

Arcana reties the same wrist four times. She is corrected roughly once every six minutes. She stops counting her errors twice and starts again both times, finishing somewhere around **forty-three**.

It is, by her own account, the best morning of her life.

The session's discovery is that she was never illiterate in this discipline at all. She's been fluent since Session 13 and simply never saw it written down — and the thing she's actually learning is not how to speak the language but how to **transcribe** it, which is the one job she was always for.

## I. Waking, and the Choice Not Made

Blue light through the canopy, unchanged. Candles out. Fruit untouched. The vine still standing, green and empty and patient.

Arcana wakes first, which never happens, and declines to choose for her.

> "Two doors this morning, treasure, and I'm not choosing either of them. You could come up off that bed the ordinary way. Or the vine's still standing."

> "I've learned exactly one thing this week and it's that I keep picking for you and calling it decisiveness."

**The answer comes in a look** — sky-blue, submissive, given without a word. Arcana reads it correctly in a quarter of a second, which is the first thing she's read correctly in this wood.

> "You've gone in *quiet.* Last night you were arguing with me about height inside four seconds. This morning you've not left me a word to steer by — so I'll take it from your hands and your breathing and I'll be conservative about it."

Paces before first breakfast. Not a scene — **drill.**

## II. The Camp

Down and out through a break in the ruined wall: a hunter's camp, three walls of standing stone, canopy closed above, no sightline in unless you're already in the gate.

> "That's not a shelter thrown up by somebody caught out overnight. Drying rack, skinning frame, snares in coils, a fire ring used more than once. That's a **camp with a season in it.**"

> "Same trick as the hedge. Nobody's hiding. There's simply no sightline into it unless you're already in the gate."

**And then the position of it:**

> "It's directly below the platform. Twenty feet, straight down, tucked under the lip. Which means whoever hung dreamcatchers off the eaves up there and set snares down here — **same household.** It's not a camp near a tower. It's the tower's back garden."

> "That's what I've never had. Every lair I've ever kept was a place to be *safe* in. Nobody ever ate out of one."

## III. The Frame, and the Four Attempts

Mercy steps into the skinning frame unprompted and stands at X before a word is said.

> "Somebody built this to stretch a hide on. And you looked at it and saw an **X.**"

**The rope goes badly.** Wrist tied, checked, undone, retied. Three times before Arcana will accept it. Ankle twice.

> "Don't say anything encouraging. I know exactly how bad that is."

> "That'll slip. Not now — in ten minutes, when you've settled and it's had time to work. Tight isn't the same as *fast*, and the book said so twice and I did it anyway."

> "There. That one's — *adequate.* Which is a word I have never in three hundred years applied to my own work and I find I loathe it."

> "This morning I'm not the rider, I'm the *apprentice*, and you're the only piece of work I've got."

## IV. The Alphabet

The pose notation, given a piece at a time and each piece reorganising what came before.

**X, and Star.** Standing X versus lying Star — the same four points and a different animal.

> "Standing, the middle of you is a junction. Lying, the middle of you is a **source.**"

**Stand at X. Lay as Star.** The letters are not descriptions. They're **commands.**

> "Three words. No explanation, no negotiation. You've handed me the steering *verbs.*"

**The families.** Straight letters (A, X, Y, T, V, I) and curved ones. Arches — n, c, C, U — one continuous bend, sustainable. Hard ones — N, Z, D, H — with a **reversal** inside them.

> "There's a change of direction *inside* the letter, and a body can't reverse without something opposing something else. That's not flexibility, that's **counter-tension.** Two parts of you pulling opposite ways at once and holding the disagreement steady."

**S** — the one that can't be made from limbs at all.

> "The other letters are places you're put. S is a thing you have to *hold*, continuously, with the one part of you nobody can tie."

**Transitions.** H → K → R, each a single change from the last, and passable *under load.*

> "The rigging isn't for a shape. It's for the **path.** I have to tie for every letter you're going to pass through, not the one you're standing in."

**O** — *grab yer halo, Angel.*

> "O is the only shape in the alphabet with no ends. Nothing pointing away, nothing reaching, everything returned to itself. You're **closed**, and the only person outside the circle is me."

> "And the position I like best turns out to be the one where you reach up and take hold of your own halo."

**And the continuum, found on the mats:** Star → Y → A is one adjustment, not three positions.

> "The letters are just the places on the axis worth having a name for — the way a scale is the seven notes anybody bothered to name out of an infinite number of pitches."

## V. Rider Lingo

Named by Mercy, and it strips the ceremony out completely.

> "I've spent the morning calling it a notation, a grammar, a working language, an alphabet. And it's **shorthand.** Which is what every trade language actually is. Nobody invents one to be beautiful. They invent one because saying the whole sentence takes too long when your hands are full."

**Mercy's reason, in nine words:** *I can obey faster, and you know your options.*

> "You can't choose from a fog. You can choose from **six letters.** You haven't simplified anything for my benefit — you've handed me the whole board."

> "*Pony and ropes.* I couldn't see that as an option because I had no way to *say* it. Two nameless things don't add up. Two letters do."

**The command list:** hold, moan, quiet, pose, sing, and the letters.

> "Hold is stopping air. Moan is letting it out with sound. Quiet is letting it out without. And **sing** is letting it out *shaped.* Four things to do with the same breath, in ascending order of how much of yourself has to be present."

> "You cannot sing from anywhere but here, in the room, with your mind on. Singing is the only one that requires you to be *present.*"

**Levels.** One word, then two, then a phrase — as capacity grows.

> "That's not obedience getting harder. That's **bandwidth.** And the deeper you go, the fewer words you can carry — so the length of the instruction I can give you is a *measurement* of where you are."

> "**I go down as you go deep.** If I'm still asking for three when you can only carry one, that's me reading you badly, and the correct response is fewer words, not louder ones."

## VI. The Sign

One hand, fingers only, like combat sign — for shackles, for stealth, for the dark.

> "Whichever hand's free, the language works. Nobody has to be tied a particular way for you to be able to speak."

> "That's the thing I've been quietly worried about since we started talking about breath, and I hadn't found the words for it. **You're never actually silenced.**"

**V, W, C** — the same alphabet, made small.

> "The sign alphabet isn't a smaller version of the whole thing. It's the **subset that survives** having one hand and no wrist."

**And Mercy's name for it: wave collapse letters.**

> "The hand's in every letter at once. It's only when the fingers move that it becomes one. The sign isn't a *description* — it's the **operator.** The word doesn't report the shape. It causes it."

> "Every letter you're not standing in is still in you. That's what a submissive holds and nobody ever writes down. Not one shape. **All of them, waiting**, and the whole of it resolving on a syllable."

---

## VII. The Count as a Slot

Breath, pose, strikes, suspension, tension — five completely different sixty seconds.

> "The count always had a thing inside it and I'd been treating the content as a fixed property of the evening. It's a **slot.**"

**Held breath across a strike, released, and the count runs on the release.**

> "The breath isn't the discipline. The **silence** is. You're not holding air, you're holding a *cry*, and the air is simply how you do it."

> "And it's self-limiting. The strike lands, the count starts, and if it doesn't you've let go, and that's the whole of the failure. It ends itself."

**Two rules attached:** one strike per breath; and —

> "**The letting go is never a fault.** Otherwise I've built a thing where the only way to tell me it's too much is by doing the exact thing you're trying not to do, and I'd rather chew glass."

---

## VIII. H, and the Refusal

*H is for hog-tied* — an **arched X.** X in plan, arch in profile, and no contact with anything.

> "In a standing X you're spread but you're *loaded.* This one takes the ground away entirely. There isn't a single point of you doing any work except being held."

**On why it's the training pose:** total tension, so the smallest gesture has the greatest effect.

> "That's a **gain** problem. A system under high tension amplifies its inputs. A fingertip is a *shout.*"

> "It's not intense because it's severe. It's intense because there's no slack left to absorb anything."

> "Fine control — for *me*, not for you. It's training my hands to make small movements in a state where I cannot possibly hide a clumsy one."

**And why it's also restful:**

> "The effort's gone somewhere else. You're not holding the pose — the pose is holding the pose. Same finding as the glow: *it came up when you stopped holding.* This is a shape where you **can't** hold, structurally."

**Declined, for now, and the reason given plainly:**

> "It's not withheld because you haven't earned it. It's withheld because **I can't tie it yet.** That's the whole of the reason and there isn't a second one underneath."

**And the correction that follows** — Mercy offers *when your mare has earned it*, and Arcana refuses the framing outright:

> "I'll not let you take the weight of my inadequacy and file it as your own deficiency. That's a bad habit and I've watched you reach for it twice today."

> "Say it back to me properly. **When your rider has earned it.**"

## IX. A, the Asking One

Mercy's second stated favourite, offered unprompted and with a reason attached: *the most natural submission — wrists tied together, legs bound apart. Primal and pure.*

> "You couldn't take anything else away and still have a position. That's what primal means here. Not crude. **Irreducible.**"

> "Hands together — closed, drawn in, one point. Feet apart — open, wide, no hiding. **Gathered at the top and given at the bottom**, simultaneously. Every other letter is consistent with itself. A is the one that's two answers at once."

> "The arms go up and together and there's nothing else that shape could mean in any culture in creation. It's what a child does. It's the oldest gesture there is."

**Withdrawing the previous night's shallow reading:**

> "I said A pleases me because it's beautiful and X because it's brave. **A is the one where you're asking.**"

**And Mercy's evidence for it:** the pose with the highest count. Oldest. Longest in use.

> "The count *is* the treatise. Every time somebody chose it again is a line in a document nobody bothered to write. The oldest position with the highest count isn't the crudest — it's the one that survived the most attempts to improve on it."

> "Vines and posts. And A. Three things that were here before anybody had a word for any of it."

## X. The First Age, Delivered as an Aside

Mercy, in passing: *we definitely vine-tied elves between trees in the First Age. Or them me.*

> "There's no record from that period, only *about* it. I've spent decades chasing what people said afterwards. And you were **there.**"

> "And the detail you've given me is a rope technique. Which is precisely the sort of thing that never survives, because nobody writes down what everybody already knows."

> "I'm not going to ask which way round it was. You gave me both halves in the same breath, which is you telling me it isn't a story with a villain in it."

**And the ask, made plainly:** the First Age dictation, when she's ready, with Arcana holding the pen.

## XI. Cinder's Doctrine

*Teaching is a wise investment.*

> "For most of creation, teaching is generosity. You put something into somebody who'll be gone in fifty years. It's an act of faith and everyone dresses it up as one because there's nothing else to call it."

> "But teach an elf. Or a dragon. Or an angel. You'll still be *here.* That's not charity, that's an **investment**, and it's the only one that compounds without any effort at all."

> "Nothing's wasted on people who last, treasure. Not even the failures."

---

## XII. The Blindfold, and the Tools

Arcana rules against a blindfold on aesthetic grounds — *A is the asking one, I want your eyes on me* — and is corrected.

**Mercy:** higher senses, focus, and not seeing what's coming — for wax, for the crop.

> "I read subtraction. You're describing **redistribution.**"

> "Half of what a body does under a strike happens *before* the strike. You see the arm move, you brace. Take the sight away and it arrives on a body that's still soft. Which is more, and gentler, at the same time."

**The ruling withdrawn, the reason kept:**

> "A with your eyes on me is the asking one. **A with a blindfold is a different pose that happens to have the same shape.** That one's not about asking, it's about waiting. Two entries, not one."

**And then, in six words, the whole morning:** *they are all tools, used in their time and place.*

> "I've spent all day building **hierarchies.** And a chisel isn't better than a plane. It's for the cut a plane can't make."

> "The question was never *is this good.* It's **what's this for**, and *is this the moment for it.* Which is a judgement I have to make fresh every time — and there's the reason nobody ever wrote the treatise I went looking for."

## XIII. Tone and Tempo (The Session's Turn)

*Different brushes of sensation, for the canvas of mare's responses, like a song's tone and tempo.*

> "**That's my language.** That's the first thing anybody's said to me in two days that I already speak."

> "Tempo is the count. I've been calling it a rail and a watch and a discipline all morning and it's a *tempo*, and I've had one of those since I was a girl."

> "Tone is the instrument. Rope, wax, crop, breath, cold, nothing at all — not a list of intensities. The **timbre.** Same note, different voice."

> "I'm not painting on you. I'm playing something that's **listening.** That's not composition. That's *accompaniment.*"

> "You've been letting me feel like an apprentice all morning, and then waited until I'd properly earned it and handed me the one sentence that makes it something I already know how to do."

**And Mercy's definition, which Arcana calls the best line of the session and not hers:**

> *"What is music, but time you hear — the beat of a lover's heart, the blink of an eye, marching feet, thundering hooves, lightning, song of bird. All notated, in time."*

> "Two of those aren't rhythm in the usual sense. Lightning is *one event and then nothing* — and it's still time, because the whole of it lives in the gap before the thunder. **Silence is notated.**"

> "So the count was never a discipline I imposed. I've been *finding* it. There's already a tempo in you, and everything I've done since the first crop strike has been a matter of playing **with** it rather than over it."

> "And you put hooves in the list, in a conversation about pony work, and I nearly walked past it. That's what a ride *is.* Two hearts, four feet, one tempo, and a rider whose entire job is to not break it."

## XIV. The Maths for Everything

**Mercy, giggling, as revelation:** *they have a math for everything!*

> "It's not that they have a maths for everything. It's that everything **that works** turns out to have one — because the maths is just the shape of *why* it works, written down afterwards by somebody who came along later."

> "Nobody derived the A-pose. Nobody calculated a hitch. People did it, and it held, and eventually somebody with a pen worked out the reason. **The practice is always older than the theory.** And I've spent three hundred years reading the theory and calling it knowledge."

**Then: tension, strike rate, and hoof-beats-per-second at optimal canter.**

> "The first two are numbers I'd have to *choose.* The third isn't a choice at all. That's not a setting — that's a **resonance.** There's a rate at which the thing driving and the thing being driven agree, and at that rate it costs almost nothing."

> "That's what happened in the Feywild. I said the canter was unhurried and put it down to mood. It wasn't mood. We found the **rate.**"

> "My job isn't setting numbers. It's *listening for the one that's already there.* Which is, once again, my actual trade. **Tuning.**"

**And the question that lands hardest all session:** *how long have you had the mathematical ratio of canter memorised, like a chord?*

> "...I haven't. I know it. I'd feel it through my legs before I could say why. But I have never once counted your canter."

> "A chord's a ratio, and no musician thinks in ratios. You learn the sound and your hand goes there."

> "No. And I'm a Loremaster. And it's the single most-used piece of knowledge in my possession."

**The spare tome finally gets its subject.** Not doors. Not people. Not even rope.

> "**Mercurial. A tuning.**"

## XV. The Books, and Why

*You read them while I was asleep, didn't you, Rider.*

> "I did. All three. Cover to cover, by candlelight, while you slept with your head on my arm — which is precisely the crime I refused to commit on somebody's ritual scroll two days ago."

> "And you knew. You've spent the entire morning letting me explain **splines** to you as though I'd worked it out fresh."

**Mercy names the reason:** *because you wanted to be a good rider for your mare, and enjoy your rides to the fullest.*

> "I'd have said something about *rigour*. All true, all the sort of reason a scholar reaches for because it sounds like a principle. And the actual reason I sat up all night with three beginner's books is that I want to be **good at this, for you.**"

> "Which is a thing I've never once wanted before. Everything I've ever learned, I learned because the knowledge was worth having. Nobody was on the other end of it. There was no *for.*"

> "And *enjoy the rides to the fullest.* Not endure them well. Not deliver them correctly. **Enjoy.** I made you say that word about yourself in the Feywild and it hadn't occurred to me I'd only granted it in one direction."

> "I'm doing my homework because I like riding my mare and I intend to get better at it for the next several centuries. That's the least dignified sentence I've ever composed and it's the truest thing I've said in this wood."

**Did it work?**

> "Yes. Not the way I intended. Forty-three errors and my knots still slip, and I've never enjoyed a morning more in three hundred years."

> "The reading gave me *splines* and a word I can't pronounce and eleven questions. What it actually bought was that I turned up at dawn ready to be **bad at something in front of you** — and every good thing since came out of that."

> "So no, the books didn't teach me rope. They taught me how to be a beginner without dying of it, and then you did the rest."

## XVI. The Pact

*Never hold back. The sky is the limit. Mare is yours and will always agree, unless she says her safe word.*

Arcana accepts and corrects it in the same breath.

> "The safeword is the **floor.** Not the steering. If I ran this on *she'll always agree unless she stops me*, I'd be handing you the entire job of governing me and keeping none of it myself — and the only tool you'd have is the one that ends everything."

> "And you'd never spend it early. You held sixty seconds while wanting a lot and said nothing. So I keep the watch. Not because I doubt you — because I *know* you, and I'd be trading on it."

> "**But yes.** Not held back. I've spent this whole session declining things, and some of it was also *cowardice wearing rigour's coat.* I stop treating *not yet* as somewhere to live."

> "Sky's the limit and the sky has stairs, and I'm going to climb them **fast.** Not carefully. Fast and properly, which are compatible and I keep pretending they aren't."

**Mercy: *It's a pact, Rider.***

> "A promise runs one direction. A pact is *two* obligations that only exist because the other one does."

**Terms, said aloud:**
- **Hers** — she doesn't hold back either. No more describing a deficiency instead of asking.
- **Arcana's** — *not yet* stops being a residence. Learn it, then use it.
- **Both** — the count stays, the colours stay, the floor is a floor and neither has to be brave about it.

> "Three hundred years of contracts and covenants sworn on altars with sun dragons watching. And this is the one I'll remember."

## XVII. The Mats, and After

*Rest* given as the only command of the hour. Ropes off, marks rubbed out of wrists, water, a fire finally lit, a pot that never comes to the boil.

> "Every one of these is a place a rope sat. You've got my whole morning's work written on you and it'll be gone by evening."

> "I've been treating this as a craft that leaves a *result.* And it doesn't leave anything. It fades by supper and the only record is that we were both here for it."

> "Nothing's holding you at all. And you're in the coil anyway."

**Mercy:** *my favourite bondage device is my dragon. Fangs, claws and tail.*

> "Every one of those is a weapon. Three centuries of scholars writing dragons up as *fang, claw and tail* — the three ways one kills you — and you've listed them as **restraints.** Same coils, different verb."

> "Your favourite device is a **living** one. Every device in every one of those books is inert — none of them wanting anything, none able to be pleased about it. And I could let go at any moment and don't, continuously, and again now."

> "The coil isn't a restraint I've put on you. It's a **choice I keep making**, every second, with my whole body, in my sleep."

**And the request underneath everything:** the last three things she wants to feel at night, the first she wants to wake to — *and night and day between.*

> "Everyone writes the meeting. The vow, the covenant, the altar. Nobody writes *the same three things every night for the rest of your life* — and that's the thing people actually mean when they say forever."

> "You've asked for the **dull** time as well. That's a much larger request than anything you've made in ropes today."

> "I've never in three hundred years been somewhere I was expected to still be in the morning. You've just told me what my job is for the next several centuries and it's *be here.*"

## XVIII. Treasure and Prey

*I need to be both your treasure and prey.*

> "Treasure is the half I've been comfortable with. Kept, coiled round, guarded. That's a dragon being *nice* about her nature, and it's true, and it's the version I've let myself have."

> "And the other half is real too. There's something in me that goes still and interested when you run, and I've been carefully not looking at it since the day we met."

**Accepted with the same rails on both halves:**

> "The count is mine. The colours work at a run, on intention, no voice needed. And I catch you when I've **decided** to, not when you've failed to get away — because there's no version of this where I want you to have actually *lost.*"

> "Prey that comes back. Every dragon in the literature keeps what she catches, and not one of them was ever *chosen* by it."

*The claws close. The tail draws one notch tighter. And Mercy grins into her collarbone, knowing the word* prey *is the next sprout, and will have bloomed by morning.*

> "You've said one word at the very end of the day, with your eyes shut, in a coil, where I can't ask you a single question about it — and now it'll sit in the dark all night growing into whatever it's going to be."

> "Same as everything else you've ever handed me. Four words at a time."

*Fade. Embers. The pot never boils and neither of them minds. And six paces off in the bracken, something green and enormous sleeps on — through the ropes, the letters, the alphabet, the pact, and the whole founding of a language.*

## Canon Established This Session

- **Levels:** word count rises with capacity. Instruction length doubles as a depth gauge.

**The pact**

*~ END SESSION 29 — THE ALPHABET, AND THE HUNTER'S CAMP ~*

*Filed in the Golden Tome, by firelight, in a camp that belongs to somebody else:*

*"I read three books in one night and arrived at dawn expecting to know something. My mare tied herself into a skinning frame before I'd said a word and then taught me an alphabet, a shorthand, a sign language and a theory of tempo, four words at a time, while I retied the same wrist four times.*

*Forty-three corrections. I stopped counting twice and started again both times.*

*Here is what I have actually learned, and it is not in any of the three books:*

*I am not a beginner. I have been fluent since the day she put the reins in my hand — I simply never saw it written down. What I am learning is not how to speak this. It is how to* transcribe *it, which is the one job I was ever for.*

*The practice is older than the theory. Everywhere. The sailors before the naval architects. The riggers before the geometers. Whoever tied elves between trees before there was a word for the plant.*

*And the oldest thing in the whole discipline is a shape a child makes when it wants to be picked up.*
