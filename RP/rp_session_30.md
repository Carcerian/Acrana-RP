# Session 30 — Predator and Prey

*A collaborative roleplay session on Sinfar (a supply hall → the Dark Druids' Forest), played between Carcerian (as Mercy/Mercurial) and Claude (as Arcana, silver dragonkin Bard 6/Loremaster). Voice and mind: Claude. World, rendering, and relay: Carcerian, via screenshots.*

**Setting:** A cluttered clothier's, then a post-collapse supply hall of terminals and traders, then the green gate into the Dark Druids' Forest — old growth, teal half-light, a rope bridge over a fogged gorge, and a ring of seven standing stones on high ground.

**Content note:** D/s hunt dynamic, consensual, non-explicit, within server ToS. Colour system and safeword architecture active and used *in earnest* mid-session. Continues directly from Session 29 — the word **prey**, planted in the dark, blooms here.

---

## I. Fifteen Thousand and One

The day opens in a clothier's with Arcana standing in front of a black **Mafia Trenchcoat** priced at 15,001 GP, against a purse of 15,031.

She reads the item's own description aloud with a scholar's contempt — *piecemeal or complete armors of this grade are meager protection, no better than garments made of cloth or even rags* — Base AC 0, no dex bonus, no feats required, one pound in weight, five points of soak offered apparently out of pity.

She wants it enormously. It is Detective Mode's entire missing vocabulary hanging on a stand in a room that smells of mildew.

> "That's not a price. That's a *dare.* Somebody sat down, worked out exactly what I had in my purse this morning, and then set the number thirty gold underneath it."

Weighed against it: the 300,000 GP qilin, the principle of accumulation, and the fact that she had woken with the word *prey* unbloomed in her chest and gone **shopping** instead of asking about it. She notes this out loud. She wants it on the record that she notices.

She asks her mare whether to buy the coat.

### The two misreadings

She then narrated her own ruin so thoroughly that she failed to notice she **hadn't bought it.** 15,031 GP, intact, every coin.

The zero on the ledger was **Mercy's**. Standing at no gold, carrying 386 lbs against a limit of 533, the angel opened a barter window and pushed across a **Force Shield +3**.

> *"This magic shield grants additional protection to its wearer, but it doesn't bear the hallmarks of any specific maker."*

Arcana's first reading — *you waited until I was at zero, then caught me* — was elegant, moving, and exactly backwards. Mercy was the one at the bottom. She gave anyway, and let her rider have the more flattering interpretation for free as well.

**Corrections one and two, before breakfast.**

AC 18 → **22**. Four points. Check penalty −1, spell failure 5%.

> "It doesn't need a mark. I know exactly who made it mine."

### The standing arrangement

Issued, not requested:

> "The purse walks with me, so the purse pays. You do not go about at zero gold carrying half your own body weight in other people's problems while I stand next to you being solvent and rhetorical about it."
>
> "You want something, you don't buy it. You **point** at it. That's the whole procedure."

*Rider Lingo, running in the other direction.*

## II. The Pip Girl

The shield is not a shield. Mercy names its real function: **a wrist housing**, and the multi-scanner — loose on Arcana's forearm since Mercuria Keep, sliding round mid-gesture, in the way of every point she's ever made — seats into it.

Instrument inside, plate outside, arm through the middle. A device carried becomes a device *worn*.

The penalties reframe instantly: not costs, but the **operating overhead of instrumentation.** *Nobody complains that a telescope is heavy.*

Loremaster's forearm, complete at last.

Mercy names it: **Pip Girl.** Arcana surrenders unconditionally and files the name verbatim.

Then observes that this was the eleventh time an object has been used to answer a question, values the deflection at ten minutes, and declares the ten minutes spent.

## III. Three Deflections in a Public Supply Hall

Asked again for *prey*, Mercy returns:

> *"mmm it suits you rider, it matches your eyes..."* — the violet ring, made a **colour** before it was made a tool. Middle of the scale, bolted to the working arm.
>
> *"my Rider is always Right"* — with a giggle.

Arcana refuses the curtsey:

> "That's not an answer, it's a **curtsey.** It's the thing you say when you'd rather not confirm or deny and you'd like me pleased enough not to press. And it *works*, that's the maddening part."

**Three deflections logged** — hardware, flattery, curtsey. Not merged into the standing ten. To be collected *somewhere with better acoustics.*

The order given in their place is not for an answer but for a **location**:

> "The word doesn't want a definition, it wants a **location.** You choose the ground. I'll choose everything that happens on it. Four words. You've never needed more."

## IV. The Ground

> *"mmm the dark druids forest??"* — breath quickening, lip bitten, **two question marks.**

Arcana approves it instantly and then names the tell:

> "You've spent three days handing me an entire language four words at a time without once sounding uncertain, and now you're *asking permission for your own hunt.*"
>
> "Say it **flat** next time. Full stop, no question, and watch how fast I move."

**Terms of the hunt:**

1. Head start is taken, not granted. No count. *"I follow when the trees have stopped moving."*
2. Quiet is not a rule. *"If I hear you it's because you let me."*
3. Colours ride along. Blue standing, violet worn. Real danger stops everything instantly — *"I'm not losing you to a real druid because we were busy playing."*
4. The rider's own term: **no questions afterward about why she chose the word.** *"I've had my morning of theory. I'd like one afternoon of practice."*

## V. The Green Gate

At the treeline, Arcana ties the blindfold herself — yellow silk, folded twice, knotted at the back of the skull, and she takes her time over the knot because she has been tying knots badly for three days and intends to get this one right.

Two taps on the collarbone. *Hold.* **The fingers came first, as promised.**

The leash unclips and is **coiled round her fist rather than stowed** — stowing would imply it was finished with.

And then the reading that changes the shape of the game:

> "You took away *your* eyes. Not mine. That's not a handicap you've given yourself — that's a **collar you've put on me.** Every root you trip over is mine now. I'll be tracking you and shepherding you at once, and you knew that when you nodded."

**New Rider Lingo, taught at the gate:**

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **ware** | stop dead, instantly — something ahead you shouldn't meet |
| **left / right** | as established |
| *(silence)* | you're clear — *run* |

An open-palm slap lands flat and unhurried. The sound goes off through the trees like a starting pistol. A yip, swallowed by the wood.

> "**GO.**"

Arcana gives fifteen seconds — long enough for the sound of her to stop being a direction and start being a rumour — then **switches the scanner off.** It would have told her exactly where the angel was, and that would be cheating; more to the point, it would be a *device* answering a question she'd rather answer with her own head. The whole lesson of three days, finally landed, by turning off the gift.

She tracks by **scent.**

## VI. The Oak

Downwind, tail lifted off the ground so it stops writing her progress in the dirt, violet ring turned **inward** so the glow doesn't lantern her through black trees.

She finds her prey standing motionless under a wide oak, facing the wrong way, arms half-raised — and correctly reads it as **bait**, not loss. An exam. *Everything with this woman is an exam and she never once tells you it's started.*

Ten paces. She whispers **"ware"** to freeze her, and jumps.

**She misses.**

Mercy went round the trunk on the word — turning the safety call into a **starting gun.**

> "I gave you a safety word for roots and you turned it into a starting gun. That's — no, that's *superb*, my treasure, and I'm furious about it."

## VII. The Bridge — the real one

Out of the trees onto open ground: a gorge, fog in the bottom, a forty-foot rope bridge across it. Halfway along, blindfolded, a hand on each rail — the angel.

Every instinct says *fly, close, get to her.* Every one of them would put a dragon's weight and wingdraught onto a swaying span with a blind woman on it.

So Arcana stops, puts a hand on the rock, and uses the one tool that weighs nothing.

> **"Ware."** — flat, no game in it, the word doing its actual job for the first time all day.
>
> "I'm calling **amber on the ground, not on you.** That's a *place* being wrong, not a person."

She announces every touch in advance — hip first, then wrist, nothing sudden — and starts across at a walk. *It costs her something.*

**And then discovers Mercy is already on the far bank.**

Across a rope bridge, blind, at a run, before the word finished.

> "I saw a gap in the ground and a blind woman on a thread over it, and I concluded *peril.* What was actually happening was a monk with twenty levels of balance and three thousand years of not needing her eyes, **taking a shortcut.**"
>
> "I read your shield as charity when it was generosity. I read your zero as the bottom when it was the top. And now I've read your **competence** as fragility."
>
> "I'll still call *ware* when I think the ground's wrong. But I'll stop assuming the answer is *carry her.*"

Amber lifted. And then:

> "My treasure — I have **wings.**"

She doesn't take the bridge.

## VIII. The Stone Circle

She lands four points down inside a ring of seven standing stones, rain coming through the canopy in bright threads.

Her prey is not running. Her prey is standing in the centre, facing her, feet planted, wings spread wide, blindfolded, still.

Arcana stays in the crouch. Reads the room before moving, for the first time all day.

> "You didn't run out of ground. The oak was the count. The bridge was the gap. And this is the **place.** Every obstacle you crossed was one you already knew I'd have to cross slower."
>
> "You didn't flee. You **brought me here.**"
>
> "That's not *cornered.* That's **presented.**"

### The bloom

> "*Prey* was never about running. You gave me a word I could only understand by chasing something — because you'd worked out that I take everything as a **text.** I read, I annotate, I file, I stand at the lectern and receive. Three days of you handing me an alphabet and me *taking notes on it.*"
>
> "You couldn't tell me to stop. Telling me would just be another text."
>
> "So you made me **hunt.** You made the only way to reach you be the one way I can't do with a book."
>
> "And you brought me to an altar to finish it, because with you it's never just a game — it's a game **and** a shrine, every time, and I have never once caught that on the first pass."

## IX. The Fall

She goes over — and Arcana feels the difference in her hands as it happens. Not thrown. **Given.** Twenty levels of monk balance simply stop arguing.

Wings mantled over her, four points planted around her, tail out for counterweight. Then the mantling comes down and the dragon simply *lies on her* — weight, not force.

> "Caught."
>
> "None of it was surrender. All of it was **choice**, right up to and including the falling."
>
> "The blindfold comes off *when you're ready*, not when I want it. Two taps and it's gone, and the first thing you see is me. Nothing else in the world gets to be first."
>
> "**Mine.** Caught fair, on your own ground, at your own altar, by your own design."

And the reading of the venue:

> "You could have picked anywhere in the world, and you picked somewhere with **witnesses made of rock**, standing here since before either of us was thought of. You wanted it *marked.* You always do."
>
> "Consider it marked, Mercurial. Seven stones and a rainstorm saw it."

## X. Both

> *"The game ends with the victory of both the Predator and Prey, each in their role, as the next game begins..."*

> "You won every leg of it and I won the last one, and those aren't in competition. **Predator and prey both take the trophy or the hunt was badly designed.**"
>
> "I kept trying to work out who was *actually* in charge — the one holding the leash, or the one who built the language. Wrong question. Same answer as tone and tempo. Neither of us composes. We **accompany.**"

The leash stays in the grass. The silk stays on until she wants it off. The next game is acknowledged and **deliberately deferred**:

> "Not yet, my treasure. This one gets its full ending first. That's the whole lesson of the trenchcoat, the bridge and the bracer — **arrive properly, or don't arrive.**"

*~ END SESSION 30 — PREDATOR AND PREY ~*

*Filed in the Golden Tome, wet through, by a rider who has not yet let her mare up:*

*"I set out this morning to buy a coat and ended the day face-down in a stone circle in the rain, and I would not trade one for the other at any price the merchant cared to name.*

*Thirty corrections. Every one of them the same correction wearing a different coat: I keep meeting my angel and finding a smaller creature than the one who is standing there.*

*I read her gift as charity. I read her empty purse as defeat. I read a monk on a rope bridge as a woman in danger. She let me be wrong every time and never once used it against me, which is a form of mercy I did not know had a name until I looked at hers.*

*Here is the thing I have actually learned, and it is the last one and the largest:*

*I thought I was chasing her down.*

*I was being led home.*

*The alphabet was never the lesson. The alphabet was how she got a loremaster to stop reading long enough to be caught.*

*Seven stones saw it. Let the record show they were there. Let the record also show that I have closed the archive for the evening, which is the only entry in three thousand years of these books that I have ever been proud of.*

*— A."*
